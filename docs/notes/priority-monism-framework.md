# Priority monism: a composite fundamental whole

Bounded investigation for #331/#325, connected to #310/#312/#319/#320. This is a
source-grounded affirmative rival map, not a new formal result or a completed
comparison of all priority-monist arguments. The material cosmos might be basic
and composite while its parts are derivative. Calling it a whole does not by
itself require that parts ground it; calling it basic does not provide a
sufficient intrinsic explanation of its existence.

## Positive account and exact open commitments

Schaffer's domain is actual material objects; deities and spirits are expressly
outside the comparison (§1.1, p.33). He assumes a cosmos with proper parts and
rejects strong composition-as-identity for the debate (pp.34–35). His `Dxy`
means x metaphysically depends on y, separate from `Pxy` (parthood).
Basicness is relative: a concrete x depends on no concrete y (§1.3, p.38).
It therefore does not assert independence from every excluded category or
identity with the repository's ordinary efficient-cause relation.

Well-founded strict priority is openly assumed (§1.2, p.37), rather than proved
against infinitism and coherentism. The tiling argument adds distinct bridges
(§1.3, pp.39–41): completeness requires covering; free recombinability and
constraints on overlapping objects support no overlap; minimal completeness and
whole-to-part duplication support the weaker no-parthood condition actually
used later. These are substantive claims about explanation, modality and
redundancy, not consequences of parthood syntax alone. In particular, rejecting
unrestricted recombinability is a principled rival response to that argument,
not automatically rejection of all fundamentality.

Under that framework the monist's ground of material objects is the composite
cosmos; parts remain real and depend on it (§1.4, pp.42–43). Pluralism assigns
basicness to multiple proper parts. Atomism adds that basics are simples;
pluralism alone does not. Neither thesis fixes every dependence relation among
derivative objects (p.44).

The exact framework commitments, retaining their source status rather than

| Foundationalism | §1.2 p.37: a strict, transitive, well-founded priority order; the assumptions are admitted rather than defended there. | Infinitist/coherentist grounding, or proof that the actual efficient-power relation is well-founded. |
| Monist basis | §1.4 pp.42–43: exactly one basic material object, the cosmos; under the stated foundationalism every proper part of u depends on u. | Every intermediate-whole edge or the absence of every derivative-to-derivative edge. |
| Tiling/explanatory rationale | §1.3 pp.39–41: completeness motivates covering; recombinability motivates no overlap; economy/minimal completeness motivates the weaker no-parthood-between-basics condition. | Every whole being basic, actual efficient-causal production of every part, or an analysis of why the cosmos exists. |

The common required rooted clause is
`∀ x, P x u ∧ x ≠ u → D x u`.
It concerns each part's dependence on **the** basic cosmos. The stronger clause
`∀ p w, p < w → D p w` concerns every intermediate whole as well. Conversely,
exactly identifying D with `D x y ↔ y = u ∧ x ≠ u` removes **all** intermediate
dependencies. Neither completion follows just from the rooted clause.
This is a scope distinction inside a fixed D and domain, not a change of
meaning that turns causal power into parthood.

A potentially misleading extra argument is already present in the witness:
§1.3 pp.40–41 says duplicating a whole fixes its parts and their intrinsic
properties/relations. Its stated use is minimal completeness and excluding
**two basics** related as whole and part. It does not infer `D p w` for every
nonbasic w and p. The independently given p.44 latitude expressly prevents
that inference from being attributed to this framework. Moreover §1.2 pp.35–36
uses mutual necessitation with asymmetric dependence to motivate the distinction
between modal covariance and priority. A universal bridge from whole-to-part
duplication to whole-to-part metaphysical dependence would be an additional
application premise, not the argument already read.

### Positive explanatory account and the Raven boundary

The strongest positive account supported by this reading is not a bare
“composition happens” assignment. A basic integrated material whole provides
the complete basis for its real derivative material parts; the completeness,
modal recombinability and economy arguments explain why basics are supposed
to provide a nonredundant covering basis. The monist allows parts without
having those parts constitute independent prior bases. Schaffer supplies a
positive foundational framework and a reasoned account of the dependence
question, rather than merely denying a parts-first conclusion. The restricted
material arena and open warrant/application of its assumptions remain visible.
Those limits do not render its reasons worthless or count as evidence against
it. No substantive objection to the permitted equal-secondary ordering itself
is identified in this selected range.

The original parts-first rival is excluded from a WF interpretation of this
atomless carrier **if** every proper part supplies a ground link of its whole.
DS-2 supplies that mathematical obstruction, not evidence that every actual
part supplies that link or that actual material reality is gunk. The full
intermediate-whole completion has a different, independently justified failure
mechanism on this carrier, described above. These are specific conditional
structural tests, not artificial parity between unspecified alternatives.
A positive parts-first account may instead defend actual atomicity, another
object domain or another dependence policy; those commitments must be compared
on their own content. Priority monism and Markosian brute composition remain
distinct accounts.

The owner's [2026-11-12 discriminator](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/formal-logos/issues/331#issuecomment-6826855278)
