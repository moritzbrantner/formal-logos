### Source warrant and noncircular use

The owner's reduction is consonant with Aquinas's wider account. ST I
q.3 a.6 ad 2 reduces principles of accidents to principles of substance;
this motivates dependence, not identity of respects. Q.3 a.1 co., second
proof, argues from first being to exclusion of potency. Q.3 a.7 co., third
proof, supports the composite-cause principle; its fourth proof runs
composition to act/potency and does not prove the converse `d8024a38`.
These provide support options, not automatic proofs of every reconstructed
premise. Source: [ST I q.3](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1003.htm),
Dominican translation, cited complete articles read on 2026-11-17; existing
Latin passage/claim authorities remain unchanged.

The strongest operative account adds that the source gives and preserves
powers **and applies them in acting**. ST I q.105 a.5 co. and ad 1–3 support
primary and secondary responsibility for one operation while retaining
proper secondary activity. This answers the objection that a common source
must replace ordinary causes. But the article applies an already established
God-description. Its God-indexed `CreatedAgentIsAppliedToActByGodReading`
cannot establish that same identification; the no-potency arguments above
do not use it. Source: [ST I q.105 a.5](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1105.htm#article5),
complete article reread on 2026-11-17; corpus passage `915df403` and
[the existing transfer analysis](causal-perfection-transfer.md) retain
their own witness and interpretation boundaries.

There is a source-based reason to keep the causal predicates distinct:
ST I q.45 a.2 ad 2 distinguishes creation from change in a pre-existing
subject. Causing esse is not automatically one more `MovedIn` event.
Source: [ST I q.45 a.2](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1045.htm#article2),
complete article read on 2026-11-17. This auxiliary reading adds no pinned

### Restored commitments and the surviving warrant question

The older all-moved weak model is not a countermodel to either completed
source bundle. Restore an actual root and the displayed
existence/composition/esse-ground inputs: if the root were one of its moved
beings, moved-to-potency would give an incoming actual ground, contradicting

its all-moved conclusion. This is a deductive restoration test, not a new
machine-checked model result; its original weak-bundle theorem remains intact.

The positive conceptual case for `d8024a38` is already developed in
`383d23ad`: an actual bearer with a real passive capacity contains both
determinate actuality and receptivity to an actuality it lacks. Under the
act/potency account these are distinct real principles of one subject,
not two independently existing objects. The uniter argument then asks
what accounts for their being one actual bearer. This explains how
potency in a different respect can lead to a question about its esse.
Merely distinguishing descriptions does not establish that constitution,
and an idle capacity is not thereby presently actualized in its own respect.

The substantive remaining comparison is the recorded **immanent-unity
account**: potential and actual principles constitute one subject
intrinsically; a cause of a change need not cause its bare esse. Its
positive distinction is constitution versus efficient production, not
efficient self-production. Aristotle H.6 and the further q.104 comparison
are assessed in
[composition-causal-scope.md](composition-causal-scope.md#source-discrimination-immanent-unity-becoming-and-continued-esse).
That account has not supplied an established uncaused actual subject with
passive potency. It challenges the universal composition-to-distinct-esse
source extension; it is not a demonstrated counterexample to the complete
source bundle. Conversely, observed organization or an accidental-state
producer does not alone establish that universal extension. The prior
`SimplePotentRoot` control in `ef2c087e` separately tests rejecting the
potency-to-composition bridge; it establishes its logical role, not the
metaphysical possibility of a simple passive root.
