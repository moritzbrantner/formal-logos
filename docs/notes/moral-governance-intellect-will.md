## Source-sensitive intellect and will tests

Aquinas's `ST` I–II q.90 aa.1–4 distinguishes an ordinance of *practical reason*, direction to a *common good*, legitimate care of the community, and *promulgation*. In q.90 a.1 ad 3, command proceeds from reason toward means to an end that is **willed**; bare sovereign desire is not law unless answerable to reason. In q.91 a.1 and q.93 a.1 he already assumes divine providential government, then describes eternal law as the ordering of divine wisdom. These passages provide a substantive conditional interpretation of **genuine practical lawmaking by this source**. They are not a deduction from any objective truth's being lawlike.

- **Ontic grounding only:** `ActualRoot(n) ∧ NecessaryAt(n) ∧ GroundsMoralOrder(n,O)` does not, by its selected meaning, contain or entail `HasIntellect(n)` or `HasWill(n)`.
- **Weak rational ordering:** `O` is intelligibly ordered, persons understand it, or `n` causally produces rational creatures. Still no entailment that `n` itself recognizes ends. One cannot transfer a property of an effect or of a norm to its cause by renaming the cause 'rational governor'.
- **Source's practical understanding:** if `n` *itself* grasps the morally relevant ends/agents and issues a practical ordinance on that understanding, an explicit recognition bridge supports `HasIntellect(n)`. This premise needs positive warrant that the *underived* source, not a later rational agent, performs that act. It must not define 'understands' as `HasIntellect` or collapse the two into a new opaque axiom.
- **Capacity-level will:** applying the *existing* `WillFollowsIntellectReading` to the same `n` supports `HasWill(n)`. The content/warrant of that q.19 a.1 premise, and the #1731 intellect bridge, stay visible; no additional attribute conclusion is manufactured.
- **Actual willing of a good:** if `n` really promulgates by taking a common end as good and deliberately ordering means toward it, q.90 a.1 ad 3 and `SCG` I.72 §6 offer a candidate **actual end-adoption** explanation beyond passive knowing. Explicitly distinguish this from a theoretical knower whose representation merely coincides with an effect. This supports, with an open intentionality-to-act interpretation, some actual willing of the governing end. It does **not** entail free selection of contingent effects, actual choice among options, `WillsGoodFor(n,r)`, universal love, veracity, or non-arbitrary priority among every competing duty. #1459 and #1737 retain actual creation/selection; #1458's `CausalExistenceWilling` and #1732 retain recipient-directed application.

`SCG` I.44 §§4,7 supplies a different candidate intellectual-cause rationale: secondary intelligent directors and determinate natural ends lead to the first cause's intellectual ordering. Its intelligent-instrument-to-primary-cause and end-institution premises need their *own* tests. Do not silently import them as moral-law premises or double-count the existing Fifth Way.

## Instrumentality, underived causation and contingent effects (2026-11-18 continuation)

This is a **scope audit** of the other proposed bridge in #1747, not another Fifth-Way/intellect proof. It uses the same informed easy-rescue norm, claimant standing, priority, root `n`, and human deliberator `h` fixed above. Add, for this audit only, that `n` is causally prior to `h`'s existence and rational powers; this extra causal-dictionary premise is not established by the weak `GroundsMoralOrder` predicate.

### The strong instrument premise in the source

[Aquinas, *SCG* I.44 §4](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/scg1044.html) uses **both** (i) the assertion that movers in the world are related to the first mover as instruments to a principal agent, and (ii) the ranking that an intellectual mover is not an instrument of a nonintellectual principal. In its selected role-sensitive reading the inference is valid:

`IntellectualMover(h) ∧ InstrumentOf(h,n) ∧ ∀x y, (IntellectualMover(x) ∧ InstrumentOf(x,y) → HasIntellect(y)) → HasIntellect(n)`.

The inference must not be called fallacious merely because those premises are disputable. But `CausesOrSustains(n,h)` is **not** `InstrumentOf(h,n)`: a lower agent may draw its capacities from a cause while setting and understanding its own proximate end. To call it an instrument of `n` *for a specified moral end* also requires end correspondence and an account of why that relation is more than mere causal ancestry. The former is satisfied in the existing q14/Fifth-Way source reading only **conditional on its own** governor and direction premises; it is not a free transfer to this moral norm.

A causal source sustains `h`'s powers both when `h` rescues and when `h` wrongfully refuses. A source's **instrument for producing capacity** is not thereby an instrument **for pursuing every end chosen by the creature**. A personal providence account may distinguish what the first source intends from what it permits; the moral-law route must still specify which end the source itself wills. Otherwise immoral use of a dependent power would wrongly be treated as the source's moral ordinance.

### Does contingency require an external intelligent tool?

A second two-world **relation-only** check held the same necessary `n` and the same intrinsic nondeterministic transition allowance `L(n) = {e₀,e₁}` in `w₀,w₁`; `e₀` occurs in `w₀`, `e₁` in `w₁`. Both actual outcomes are permitted. This finite assignment shows why `n`'s necessity does not, without a **complete necessitating-output premise**, make a unique outcome necessary. A noncognitive fundamental power could be *proposed* to yield an intelligible but contingent outcome without another mind using it as an instrument. This is a **schematic rival**, not an established metaphysical account, numerical chance theory or explanation of why precisely `e₀` occurred.

The rival therefore still owes an account of the realized token and of substantive norm authority; the personal alternative likewise owes a contrastive account of why this act was chosen under nonnecessitating reasons. Neither an opaque stochastic draw nor an opaque `Chooses(n,e₀)` field earns explanatory credit merely from consistency.
