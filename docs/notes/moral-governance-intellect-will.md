## Source-sensitive intellect and will tests

Aquinas's `ST` I–II q.90 aa.1–4 distinguishes an ordinance of *practical reason*, direction to a *common good*, legitimate care of the community, and *promulgation*. In q.90 a.1 ad 3, command proceeds from reason toward means to an end that is **willed**; bare sovereign desire is not law unless answerable to reason. In q.91 a.1 and q.93 a.1 he already assumes divine providential government, then describes eternal law as the ordering of divine wisdom. These passages provide a substantive conditional interpretation of **genuine practical lawmaking by this source**. They are not a deduction from any objective truth's being lawlike.

- **Ontic grounding only:** `ActualRoot(n) ∧ NecessaryAt(n) ∧ GroundsMoralOrder(n,O)` does not, by its selected meaning, contain or entail `HasIntellect(n)` or `HasWill(n)`.
- **Weak rational ordering:** `O` is intelligibly ordered, persons understand it, or `n` causally produces rational creatures. Still no entailment that `n` itself recognizes ends. One cannot transfer a property of an effect or of a norm to its cause by renaming the cause 'rational governor'.
- **Source's practical understanding:** if `n` *itself* grasps the morally relevant ends/agents and issues a practical ordinance on that understanding, an explicit recognition bridge supports `HasIntellect(n)`. This premise needs positive warrant that the *underived* source, not a later rational agent, performs that act. It must not define 'understands' as `HasIntellect` or collapse the two into a new opaque axiom.
- **Capacity-level will:** applying the *existing* `WillFollowsIntellectReading` to the same `n` supports `HasWill(n)`. The content/warrant of that q.19 a.1 premise, and the #1731 intellect bridge, stay visible; no additional attribute conclusion is manufactured.
- **Actual willing of a good:** if `n` really promulgates by taking a common end as good and deliberately ordering means toward it, q.90 a.1 ad 3 and `SCG` I.72 §6 offer a candidate **actual end-adoption** explanation beyond passive knowing. Explicitly distinguish this from a theoretical knower whose representation merely coincides with an effect. This supports, with an open intentionality-to-act interpretation, some actual willing of the governing end. It does **not** entail free selection of contingent effects, actual choice among options, `WillsGoodFor(n,r,_)`, universal love, veracity, or non-arbitrary priority among every competing duty. #1459 and #1737 retain actual creation/selection; #1458's `CausalExistenceWilling` and #1732 retain recipient-directed application.

`SCG` I.44 §§4,7 supplies a different candidate intellectual-cause rationale: secondary intelligent directors and determinate natural ends lead to the first cause's intellectual ordering. Its intelligent-instrument-to-primary-cause and end-institution premises need their *own* tests. Do not silently import them as moral-law premises or double-count the existing Fifth Way.

### The strong instrument premise in the source

[Aquinas, *SCG* I.44 §4](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/scg1044.html) uses **both** (i) the assertion that movers in the world are related to the first mover as instruments to a principal agent, and (ii) the ranking that an intellectual mover is not an instrument of a nonintellectual principal. In its selected role-sensitive reading the inference is valid:

`IntellectualMover(h) ∧ InstrumentOf(h,n) ∧ ∀x y, (IntellectualMover(x) ∧ InstrumentOf(x,y) → HasIntellect(y)) → HasIntellect(n)`.

The inference must not be called fallacious merely because those premises are disputable. But `CausesOrSustains(n,h)` is **not** `InstrumentOf(h,n)`: a lower agent may draw its capacities from a cause while setting and understanding its own proximate end. To call it an instrument of `n` *for a specified moral end* also requires end correspondence and an account of why that relation is more than mere causal ancestry. The end-correspondence condition is satisfied in the existing q14/Fifth-Way source reading only **conditional on its own** governor and direction premises; it is not a free transfer to this moral norm.

### A source-motivated hierarchy that is not one scalar

[Aquinas, *ST* II–II q.26 aa.1–2, 6–7, 9](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/3026.htm) gives a **positive structured answer** to how goods are ordered **within theological charity**. Under the explicitly Thomistic premise that God is charity's ultimate end and the principle of its good, goods are ordered by their relation to that end. This charity account (which presupposes friendship in God, grace and participation in beatitude) distinguishes (i) what good the lover wishes for another, including degrees of goodness/virtue, from (ii) intensity of love and acts of care tied to the lover's relation to another. It also distinguishes care due to children from honor due to parents. A higher *object-directed* value is not a universal, unconditional priority in every practical beneficence decision. In q.26 a.1 ad3, reason orders and charity as appetite is ordered; this directly concerns the **human agent exercising charity**, not the first source's own intellectual act. In a.6, Aquinas additionally appeals to the claim that natural/charitable inclinations flow from divine wisdom. That is a substantial **source-wisdom** premise of his explanation, not an inference from the existence of any hierarchy.

**Fixed overlap case:** The actual rational parent `h` can provide one equal, immediate benefit either to `h`'s own dependent child `c` or to an unrelated, notably virtuous stranger `s`. The **Thomistic q.26 application additionally assumes `h` exercises charity**; the secular profiles analyze the same outward action and parent–child facts without accepting that theological premise. The options are stipulated equal in immediate need, benefit, timing, and physical cost; there is no promise to `s`, sanction, emergency disparity, or evidence of differing basic dignity. The selected care relation and stranger's greater virtue are descriptive inputs. No all-things-considered duty verdict or superiority of overall human worth is derived merely from them.

- The q.26 object-related comparison can prefer wishing a **greater spiritually appropriate good** for `s` in virtue of the selected goodness relation.
- The same text's **charity-internal** relationship-sensitive comparison can prefer **more intense affection and the ordinary practical care** for `c`, given the dependent relation. At equal material need, this supplies a positive *candidate* reason to aid `c`; a definite all-things-considered duty needs the relevance and priority of that relation in the case.
- The case holds equal dignity fixed independently; q.26 a.6 ad1 preserves equal **generic benevolence** while allowing different intensity and particular beneficence. The two opposite comparisons have **different objects**, so they are not moral contradictions or a total ranking of persons. In a high-urgency reversal the care tie-breaker need not remain decisive; #1505 already tests urgency versus special responsibility and is not repeated here.

### Weaken the charity premise for one natural-law good (q.94 source follow-up)

The previous case deliberately assumed `Charity(h)` to apply *ST* II–II q.26. There is a **narrower, distinct support option** for the natural-law content of offspring education that does not require that theological virtue. In [*ST* I–II q.94 a.2](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/2094.htm), Aquinas places the education of offspring among goods implicated by natural-law inclinations, and says the first practical precept grounds a plurality of more specific precepts. This is an explicit **human practical-reason** reading: inclinations fall under the natural law insofar as ordered by reason (a.2 ad2). It is not yet a claim that the first necessary source `n` deliberates about each case.

**Small premise replacement, not a replacement of the whole q.26 account:**

- The q.26-internal route to a special reason to care for `c` draws on `Charity(h)`, parent–child relation, charity's ordered end, and relevant particular application.
- For **the bounded content that educating offspring is a genuine natural-law good**, q.94 offers a separate route through `RationalNature(h)`, `ParentOf(h,c)`, the natural-law good of educating offspring, and reason's governance of inclinations. No `Charity(h)` is required for *that* content. An agent-indexed obligation, `ReasonToAct(h,a)` or `Aid(c) ≻ Aid(s)` still requires due-good/justice, applicability and any conflict-priority principles; it is **not derived** from the q.94 list by itself.
- In q.94 a.4 Aquinas distinguishes stable general principles from context-sensitive practical conclusions that may fail in unusual conditions. Therefore even the natural-law support option does not supply an absolute child-over-stranger rule for unequal urgency, nor a global single ranking of moral goods.

The original equal-need help case and the positive natural-goodness/robust-realist controls stay fixed; q.94's education example does not automatically apply to every form of emergency aid. ## Who can release a promise, and what ends at expiry? (2026-11-25)

A promise lets us ask *whose choice makes a difference* to what one person owes another. The earlier rescue fixed the demand throughout. The [conflicted guardian case](natural-goodness-function-and-authority.md#next-discriminating-test-conflicted-guardian-and-protective-stranger) varied representation of a person with a pre-existing protective claim. Here competent parties voluntarily create a claim to a specific performance, can end it early, and have set a term under which it lapses. The historical point of comparison is Hart's example of promising one person to care for a third, and his description of the promisee's “temporary authority or sovereignty in relation to some specific matter.” The deadline, substitute speaker and metaphysical comparison below are analyst additions. [H. L. A. Hart, “Are There Any Natural Rights?”, pp. 180, 183–84](https://philosophyandlaw.org/Class%20Readings/Hart/Are%20There%20Any%20Natural%20Rights_Hart.pdf) [Assessment record](../../corpus/attempts/55bc6a0c-7cf3-4a97-a67d-e9ce755f4cad.json)

**Fix the undertaking.** A voluntarily promises C to keep a place in A's workshop available for visitor V until noon. C accepts on those terms. B witnesses the exchange. All are informed adults, the place is A's to offer, and V acquires no independent entitlement by reliance or agreement. Everyone knows who spoke and what was agreed. The place would be just as useful to V after noon. Hold its value, availability, cost, the parties' interests and all other obligations fixed. Consider the claim to *continued availability* while A has complied throughout. This avoids confusing expiry with forgiveness of an earlier breach. Fix also the same actual necessary source n distinct from the parties, in the weak grounding sense above, throughout the comparison.

| History | Before noon, after the indicated event | From noon, with no further act |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No one releases A | A owes C continued availability for V. | The agreed period has run. |
| C knowingly tells A “I release you from that promise” | A can close the place without wronging C on this undertaking. | The claim has already ended. |
| B uses those same words on B's own behalf, without authorization from C | A still owes C continued availability for V. | The agreed period has run. |

These verdicts depend on an explicit moral premise: a permissible, informed assurance freely offered to a particular person and accepted on stated terms can give that person control over the specified performance. One reason to accept it is that A offered C the ability to settle the matter for V. Giving B the same power would defeat that assurance even where B predicted everyone's interests correctly. C can ask A to perform **because A undertook this toward C**. A bystander can remind A of it, and V benefits if A complies. Neither fact by itself gives its bearer the power A offered C. This is prima facie warrant for the premise in this ordinary case. There is a practical reason for recognizing such a power at all: people can give one another a secure option to plan around, while leaving the recipient free to give it back. A forecast that A will keep the place open can be equally reliable yet gives C no such choice. The value of that option motivates the general principle; the further judgment that C can hold A answerable for it remains substantive. A further check changes just the authorization: if C can delegate the release and authorizes B to act for C, B's communicated release can work on the same principle. Thus an explanation keyed simply to the physical speaker's identity also misses the contrast.

The release is an exercise of practical understanding and choice by **C**: C grasps the outstanding commitment and intentionally gives up the claim. A and C had earlier settled its beneficiary, content and term. Their acts do more than detect a ranking of antecedent benefits. At noon in the first and third histories there is no fresh release, tacit consent or decision to withdraw. Even if C now wants the reservation extended, that wish cannot unilaterally extend A's undertaking. Expiry applies the term A and C already accepted. It ends only this prospective promissory claim. V's interests can still be reasons to keep the place open. These differences concern what is owed even if A in fact leaves the place open in every history.

| Account | What explains C's release, B's failure and expiry on the same facts? | What must be warranted? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Necessary norms plus second-personal authority | A standing principle gives competent persons a limited power to undertake commitments. Their voluntary exchange vests this claim in C. The holder can release it, the bystander lacks title, and the agreed term fixes its duration. | Authority to undertake and release can be a basic normative fact. Reciprocity and control over one's own permissible projects motivate it. Mere ability to utter or understand a promise does not confer the power. |
| Natural goods and practical reason | Reliable coordination and room to plan explain why people can reasonably value giving C control. A's assurance specifies whose plans have that protection and for how long. | A justice or assurance principle must make that control *owed to C* even without material reliance. A calculation using only V's fixed benefit gives identical answers for C and B. |
| Eternal-law governance | A wise lawgiver can institute a stable power for people to bind themselves for their shared good. Its exercise by C and its lapse under the original terms can both be genuine under one standing ordinance. No separate divine decree per release or deadline is needed. | That ordinance must actually come from n, with justified jurisdiction over these powers. Its positive attraction is a common rationale for the existence, purpose and limits of rational powers **if** their intentional institution by n has other warrant. |

**Provisional judgment.** There is positive evidence for personal governance of *this commitment*: even with the same possible benefit and the same words, what A owes tracks an entitled person's decision. An explanation restricted to the benefit cannot recover the difference. All three fuller accounts can. Finite agents supply the causal history of the undertaking and communication; the standing principle supplies their normative significance. No impersonal principle has to cause a speech act. Together these suffice for the three local verdicts conditional on actual standing. The strongest surviving replacement for an ultimate legislator is therefore finite authority under an impersonal principle that permits people to confer this limited control. Both that account and eternal law give a reason for honoring the specified holder and term. The case favors neither account of **why that general power exists**. Requiring every rightful power to be granted by a further will would apply to the proposed first legislator too unless there is a reason why n, specifically, has original jurisdiction. A primitive standing principle and an independently defended original divine jurisdiction are both live starting points.

**Connection to the same source.** If n instituted this power by practically representing its good and intentionally ordering persons toward it, the relevant understanding and end-adoption belong to n even when C alone exercises the private power of release. In the analyst comparison, the undertaking confers the limited power on C, given the standing premise. C exercises it in the second history by communicating the release. Causal dependence of A and C upon n supplies no instance of the former act. Assuming that n grounds the whole moral order *by its own ordinance* adds that premise; the impersonal account contests that stronger reading. The #1733 [benevolent-governance result](natural-goodness-function-and-authority.md#new-formal-connection-at-t5-benevolent-governance-lord_3) also uses antecedent willing of creaturely good, and its dominion endpoint concerns authority to dispose. Reading it backwards to supply actual willing or treating dominion as directed moral jurisdiction would beg the question here.

The [Stage Two consumer](../../TruthCore/Results/Case/Catholicism/StageTwo/ClassicalAttributesThroughGroupTwo/Challenge.lean) takes both universal attribute claims as explicit inputs alongside the other classical-attribute premises. For a particular n, even a successful intellect attribution still needs a way of identifying **each** relevant GOD_4 bearer with n, or of repeating the argument for each such bearer. Merely exhibiting an intellectual person cannot supply that input. The [existing q.19 result](../../TruthCore/Results/Causality/LocalTeleologyGodIntellect/Challenge.lean) then gives universal capacity for will under the intellect-to-will reading. It does not assert actual promulgation. The promise case adds a warrant comparison to these routes and no formal premise change.

**Next source-identity test for #1747/#1460.** During the unreleased promise, a speaker purporting to act for the source that continuously sustains A and C tells A that A may close the workshop. C objects. Hold the general power to promise, C's standing and the relevant goods fixed. Could dispensing A extinguish what is owed to C, as distinct from preventing performance or giving A an overriding reason to close? Test two independent matters: what would justify a reserved power of dispensation over a private claim, and what would identify the author of this directive with n, or its speaker as authorized by n? A lawgiver might institute a protected private power without reserving any such dispensation. Total dependence alone settles neither question, and stipulating that n sent the message would already grant source cognition. Evidence for the speaker’s authority and the directive’s origin in n’s own deliberate act would support practical cognition in n on that occasion. Justified dispensation would also establish jurisdiction over this claim. An impersonal rule could grant n that jurisdiction. To attribute the institution of the promissory power itself to n, one would need grounds for tracing the creation of the general power, as distinct from the exercise of an office under an existing rule, to n’s own intention.

### Working vocabulary and rival profiles

Reuse the existing [intellect definition](../../corpus/definitions/intellect.json)
(`88bc0655`, HAS_INTELLECT_1) and [will definition](../../corpus/definitions/will.json)
(`7c64b499`, HAS_WILL_1): intellectual cognition, not mere sensory or

Do not modify their meanings or make the existing will-follows-intellect

For this comparison only, propose:

- **Personal source (capacity sense):** the same source intrinsically has
  intellect and will. This is a conjunctive classification, not a definition
  of a Trinitarian person, a separate divine being, embodiment, temporal
  deliberation, emotional life, omniscience or free creation.
- **Strictly impersonal source:** the same source lacks both intrinsic
  intellectual cognition and intellectual appetite. The negation of the
  personal conjunction is broader: intellect without will and any other
  partial profile must be handled rather than silently excluded. The existing
  intellect-to-will premise, if warranted, excludes the intellect-only profile.
- **Purpose in the effect:** an intelligible end-relative ordering of an
  activity, capacity or nature. Distinguish proper end/function from an
  evaluator's externally assigned goal, mere stable correlation and the
  actual realized outcome.
- **Operative intention:** the source itself apprehends an end as good, adopts
  that end as to-be-realized (or maintained), and its adoption guides this
  productive exercise. This does not require a temporal transition, an
  intrinsic change, a unique optimal outcome or that the source lacks the good.
- **Personal creation:** the source produces the selected actuality through
  its own operative intention, not merely a personal source whose production
  bypasses its intellect and will.
- **Normative ground:** what makes a norm or standing obtain. **Original
  jurisdiction:** a rightful power to settle a specified normative matter
  not delegated by a prior holder. **Institution:** making a standing power
  available, as distinct from exercising it. **Authorial institution:**
  institution effected through a source's own operative intention; this is a
  stronger hypothesis than grounding or institution simpliciter.

Separate the existence modality of the bearer, modality of the productive
exercise, intrinsic possession, actual uptake, and normative standing. A
necessitating impersonal producer, a nonnecessitating indifferent producer, a
nonnecessitating objectively good-sensitive producer, a personal but
nonoperatively producing source, and a personal rational author have different
commitments. A good-sensitive propensity is a serious positive rival rather
than a synonym for indifferent chance. Necessary production does not by itself
exclude intention, and contingent production does not by itself entail it.
Where a functionalist claims that the operative organization itself constitutes
genuine intellectual cognition, test that claim rather than stipulating the
mechanism is impersonal. These proposed labels add no corpus definitions until
CLI reuse and semantic challenge have been completed.

### What the standard does, and who acts for it

Four facts must remain separate in applying that result. The subject's
truth-apt capacity has a success condition; n causes or sustains the
subject and capacity; an objectively warranted response is good or
reason-giving for the subject; and some agent actually apprehends that
reason and acts because of it. Neither causal origination nor the first
two facts alone supplies the latter two. Conversely, a norm's application
and an agent's uptake of it are distinct: having a reason need not mean
that the person is presently reasoning from it. The source-level claim
adds a further bearer, n, whose uptake is at issue.

The [rational-basing investigation](epistemic-underminer-boundaries.md#rational-basing-the-particular-transition-must-exercise-the-capacity)
offers a positive local test: available content, an intelligible
procedure, its actual contribution to this conclusion, and its place in
the person's reconsiderable activity. It also explains how someone can
understand and apply a defective procedure. Adequate evidence and an
undefeated rule remain additional conditions of warrant. An agent who
understands why the leftover matters and actually uses it can thereby
correct this inference. The mere possession of that understanding, or
its occurrence somewhere in a belief's causal history, does not establish
that it guided the token correction. This is the same distinction between
standing cognition and operative uptake required at n; it gives no
transfer from cognition in x to cognition in n.

The [conceptual-role and function comparison](self-undermining-views.md#what-the-existing-authority-evidence-actually-excludes)
already preserves objective defect without treating a function standard

For **jurisdiction**, return to the existing promise rather than adding

### The strongest constitutions at the same scope

**Personal constitution.** The source's nature is essentially good; its
wisdom apprehends that good and the recipient-relative forms in which it
can be communicated. The productive act is specified by that apprehended
good. Thus a real distinction between knowledge and error, and between
well-founded and defective inquiry, can belong to the content the source
intends rational creatures to attain. On this account the standard is
neither invented by whatever gets produced nor imposed by a superior
legislator. Its epistemic independence from a creature's answer is
compatible with its ontic grounding in the same n.

This proposal explains a joint fit: the question has a determinate
content, the subject can understand it, evidence bears on its answer,
and a suitable procedure can recover a missed distinction. An
apprehended end can intelligibly specify those relations together.
Ordinary content-guided correction gives a positive analogy for that
kind of explanation, rather than just a label for matching outputs.
Applying it to original production still requires warrant that **n's**
apprehension actually specifies the giving. The granted goodness of the
end is not supplied by the fact that n thinks about it; actual self-love
alone does not show that this recipient good is the operative object.
Likewise, essential goodness, wisdom and the creator–creature relation
can be proposed as the ground of original rightful jurisdiction, but
knowing what is correct does not itself confer that title. The
source-institution and jurisdiction bridges remain distinct.

**Noncognitive constitution.** The source's intrinsic generative nature
is directed to genuine rational fulfillment, including the relations
between capacities, objects and evidence that fulfillment requires.
It produces real thinkers, not merely successful report generators.
Their capacity to assess the unchanged question has a constitutive
success condition: a total missing the leftover is not an accurate
count of this collection. The corresponding epistemic good and reason
may be defended as natural constitutive facts, or retained as irreducible
normative facts with direct or cumulative warrant. Neither move equates
correctness with whichever belief the causal process happens to produce.
The source provides the actual productive realization; a normative
truth is not asked to act as an additional efficient producer.

Two scopes of this reply must not be conflated. The weaker reply leaves
necessary normative truths independent of n while treating n as the
concrete productive source. The existing
[`ActualRoot`](../../TruthCore/Causality/NecessaryRootFork.lean) alone does
not exclude that independence: it excludes actual concrete grounds in
its supplied relation, not every abstract standard or unmodeled
explanation. If the comparison additionally requires n to
ground the whole relevant normative order, citing an independent
truth does not meet that stronger demand. The stronger impersonal
proposal instead makes the original appropriateness and its productive
relevance constitutive of n's own nature, with norms applying to the
recipient in virtue of the recipient's actual nature and relations.
This is a substantive proposed constitution, not a result derived from
the bare fact that n causes x. It needs a defensible account of that
same-source ground just as the personal proposal needs one; no causal
to normative-grounding transitivity is assumed here.

The concrete correction favors a content-sensitive standard over
output-indexed correctness. It does not discriminate those two strong
constitutions, both of which preserve the error. A function account
that supplies only a neutral success ranking is insufficient for the
**granted objective good**; the fuller noncognitive account explicitly
retains that normative content. A personal account that supplies only
awareness of the standard is similarly insufficient for **operative
productive guidance**. These are identified omissions, not failures
attributed to the strongest accounts that repair them. Neither complete
ultimate constitution has received a joint metaphysical model here.

### Independence, identity and the precise remaining bridge

No account earns an explanation by saying that an answer is correct
because it is correct, or that n has jurisdiction because n decrees
that it has jurisdiction. But one bearer can instantiate distinct
facts: having an essentially good nature, understanding a particular
good, and acting for that understood good are different claims. Their
common bearer does not turn every proposed explanation between them
into `p` grounding `p`. Conversely, if divine simplicity is expressed
by property or reality identity, that identity alone gives no reflexive
causal or grounding arrow. An asymmetric ground explanation must state
which facts it relates and defend that direction; an essential identity
claim may instead be offered as a constitutive stopping point. Neither
proposal is ruled out just because it has no further external ground.

The standard has priority **as what makes the particular answer
correct**. It has authority for this subject only under the relevant
normative commitment, retained in the present comparison. It functions
as the source's **operative reason** only if the source apprehends and
acts for it. Promoting the first priority into the third supplies the
disputed cognitive condition, rather than deriving it. A demand that
all standards be intentionally instituted would also leave the first
institutor's legitimate standard unexplained unless a noninstituted
constitutive norm is admitted. Admitting that stopping point for n
does not by itself establish its noncognitive counterpart, but prevents
rejecting the latter merely because its standard is original.

**Provisional assessment.** Independence from a produced answer is
warranted by the preserved-content correction. It vindicates the
correctness distinction used in this inquiry, but does not establish a
standard ontically outside n or an intellectual owner of that standard
at n. The positive personal account can make goodness internal and
apprehended goodness operative without a superior lawgiver. The strong
noncognitive account retains the same objective standards and
genuine rational recipients; its proposed original constitution is
not defeated by this inquiry test. The previous promise/expiry and
#1500/#1733 assessments are unchanged. This is a narrower judgment
about the standard-to-author inference, not explanatory equivalence
of complete worldviews.

**Failed approaches and reopening.** Equating correctness with production
fails the executed pair-count case. Inferring jurisdiction from accurate
understanding fails the existing claimant/bystander contrast. Transferring
the creature's reasoning to its cause changes the bearer; appealing to
the nihilism retorsion changes the rival. Treating a standard internal
to n as automatically self-caused confuses identity with grounding.
No further output-equivalent example would resolve these same gaps.
Reopen this branch with a substantive constitutive argument that this
original normative specification requires **n's** apprehension, or with
evidence that the relevant norm was actually instituted through n's
reason-guided act. A claimed intervention must establish its source
and rightful scope, not merely its causal effectiveness. Conversely,
a worked account of how n's noncognitive intrinsic nature grounds the
granted good and its actual normative application at the full agreed
scope would strengthen that rival. A discovery that such an account
borrows a distinct external ground, contradicts the fixed simplicity
