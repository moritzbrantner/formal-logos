## One fixed discrimination case

At the selected actual world `w₀`, an informed rational agent `h` can prevent serious injury to person `r` at negligible cost, without sanction, reciprocity or expected social gain. **For comparison only**, fix the same candidate moral verdict `h ought to rescue r`, `r`'s standing to demand rescue, and priority of rescue over `h`'s trivial diversion. This is a selected substantive moral input; no model or source here independently proves that verdict or priority.

Also fix one necessary, underived candidate `n` and stipulate the same `GroundsMoralOrder(n, O)` relation throughout. Do not construe this relation as cognition or promulgation. The profiles change only **how** the agent-level authority, recognition and source-level ordering are explained. Holding the *label* and extension of a **weak, uninterpreted** grounding relation fixed does not show that Wielenberg, Boyd, Darwall or Aquinas endorses it as a full metaphysical account. In particular, Wielenberg's primitive necessary moral truths can **reject** the stronger premise that one source `n` grounds the entire authoritative order. The A/B/D controls show non-entailment in the weak shared signature; they are not countermodels of a strong authored-source premise or complete historical worldviews.

## Source-sensitive intellect and will tests

Aquinas's `ST` I–II q.90 aa.1–4 distinguishes an ordinance of *practical reason*, direction to a *common good*, legitimate care of the community, and *promulgation*. In q.90 a.1 ad 3, command proceeds from reason toward means to an end that is **willed**; bare sovereign desire is not law unless answerable to reason. In q.91 a.1 and q.93 a.1 he already assumes divine providential government, then describes eternal law as the ordering of divine wisdom. These passages provide a substantive conditional interpretation of **genuine practical lawmaking by this source**. They are not a deduction from any objective truth's being lawlike.

- **Ontic grounding only:** `ActualRoot(n) ∧ NecessaryAt(n) ∧ GroundsMoralOrder(n,O)` does not, by its selected meaning, contain or entail `HasIntellect(n)` or `HasWill(n)`.
- **Weak rational ordering:** `O` is intelligibly ordered, persons understand it, or `n` causally produces rational creatures. Still no entailment that `n` itself recognizes ends. One cannot transfer a property of an effect or of a norm to its cause by renaming the cause 'rational governor'.
- **Source's practical understanding:** if `n` *itself* grasps the morally relevant ends/agents and issues a practical ordinance on that understanding, an explicit recognition bridge supports `HasIntellect(n)`. This premise needs positive warrant that the *underived* source, not a later rational agent, performs that act. It must not define 'understands' as `HasIntellect` or collapse the two into a new opaque axiom.
- **Capacity-level will:** applying the *existing* `WillFollowsIntellectReading` to the same `n` supports `HasWill(n)`. The content/warrant of that q.19 a.1 premise, and the #1731 intellect bridge, stay visible; no additional attribute conclusion is manufactured.
- **Actual willing of a good:** if `n` really promulgates by taking a common end as good and deliberately ordering means toward it, q.90 a.1 ad 3 and `SCG` I.72 §6 offer a candidate **actual end-adoption** explanation beyond passive knowing. Explicitly distinguish this from a theoretical knower whose representation merely coincides with an effect. This supports, with an open intentionality-to-act interpretation, some actual willing of the governing end. It does **not** entail free selection of contingent effects, actual choice among options, `WillsGoodFor(n,r)`, universal love, veracity, or non-arbitrary priority among every competing duty. #1459 and #1737 retain actual creation/selection; #1458's `CausalExistenceWilling` and #1732 retain recipient-directed application.

`SCG` I.44 §§4,7 supplies a different candidate intellectual-cause rationale: secondary intelligent directors and determinate natural ends lead to the first cause's intellectual ordering. Its intelligent-instrument-to-primary-cause and end-institution premises need their *own* tests. Do not silently import them as moral-law premises or double-count the existing Fifth Way.

## Finite structural controls (not Lean certificates)

A finite valuation tested without a repository Lean toolchain used `{n,h,r,g}` as possible beings, the same actual world and rescue case, `ActualRoot(n)`, `NecessaryAt(n)`, `GroundsMoralOrder(n,O)`, the identical obligation, standing and priority in every profile. Human cognition was present. Five omission observations were checked directly as Boolean predicates:

1. With `HasIntellect(n) = HasWill(n) = false`, every fixed root/grounding/norm fact remains true. Thus the thin source/grounding base does **not** force either attribute in this interpretation.
2. A secondary rational `g ≠ n` can govern, with `HasIntellect(g)` true and `HasIntellect(n)` false, while `n` still grounds the identical order. Separate governor/root existentials do **not** establish identity.
3. `HasIntellect(n)` true and `HasWill(n)` false preserves the base and source cognition **when the q.19 will-follows bridge is omitted**. This isolates the role of that existing premise; it is not a counterexample to the Thomistic argument that *includes* it.
4. `HasIntellect(n)`, `HasWill(n)`, and a source-level intentional ordering can all hold with `WillsGoodFor(n,r)` false. Universal recipient-specific benevolence does not follow from a unary will attribute or from governing an order alone.
5. A populated profile with both cognitive and intentional governance at `n`, `HasIntellect(n)` and `HasWill(n)` satisfies the proposed conditional route. This witnesses nonvacuity of the *stipulated* predicates, not metaphysical possibility or theological truth.

The source-identity omission and object-of-will omission are informative; a Lean theorem that merely applies `GovernsByIntellect → HasIntellect` after making its antecedent true by definition would be an invariant-16 transcription. No theorem, new claim, source record, premise or frontier status is altered.

## Provisional comparison and next discriminating test

**Provisional judgment:** there is a coherent and source-motivated *conditional* route from genuinely **authored practical moral law by the necessary source** to its intellect and its exercised orientation toward a good. It is promising for same-source explanatory unification across value, reason, ends, and moral governance. Grounding moral truth, objective lawlikeness, intelligible ordering and recognition *by creatures* are weaker and do not carry source intellect. Capacity-level will is already covered by the existing q.19 route. A combined impersonal necessary-realist account with irreducible practical standards and second-personal claimant authority is the strongest surviving rival for this fixed case: its account can preserve binding force, fallible recognition and case priority without assigning intellect to `n`. It still owes a warrant for the substantive standards/standing; this is not a logical inconsistency, and C likewise owes wise-jurisdiction and source-intentionality warrants.

**Smallest next test, no duplicate generic attribute theorem:** select one existing moral-order or due-goodness statement from #1460/#1733 and reconstruct the **actual promulgation/adoption of that very norm**. Compare the *same* root `n`, case, norms and grounds against a secondary rational governor `g` who recognizes/announces the norm without `n` doing so. A successful discriminating bridge must show why ultimate explaining/causing that norm entails *its intentional institution by n*, or offer a cumulative IBE that makes the same-source interpretation preferable without claiming formal entailment. Reuse #1731's existing same-witness governor adapters and #1459's actual-willing distinction; then challenge source-identity, law/jurisdiction and end-adoption separately. The old result consumers and all root closure classifications remain unchanged.
