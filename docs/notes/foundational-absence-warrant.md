The rival accounts are fixed as analyst profiles (attribution deferred):

- **Primitivism.** Some modal facts about existence are basic and have no
  actual ground.
- **Combinatorialism.** Possibilities are recombinations of actual elements.
  - Its *unrestricted* form frees the absence of every individual (the
    unrestricted free absence of `03e1f49f`; certified difference `542c595e`,
    whose extra content is exactly the exclusion of any concretum existing at
    every accessible world).
  - Its *restricted* form frees only candidates that could be absent.
  - Lewisian counterpart theory (listed among the rivals in C2 note §8) is
    treated here as falling under recombination through its patchwork
    principle (navigation only). It is **not assessed separately**.
- **Existence-conditional essentialism (EC).** Necessity is truth in virtue of
  essence (essence-first), and essences yield only conditional truths,
  "necessarily, if `x` exists, then `x` is F". *Unrestricted* EC: no existence
  is unconditionally necessary. *Concreta-restricted* EC: the restriction
  applies only to the essences of concreta, so abstracta may exist necessarily.
- **Unconditional essentialism.** Some essence can make an existence
  unconditionally necessary.
- **Hybrid.** Powers ground the possibilities that changes realize. Cross-world
  existence and absence get one of the non-power accounts above.

## 2. Test 1: evidence for the possibility itself

### 2.1 Candidate data for "`n` could be absent"

**(a) Subtraction (Baldwin).** SEP *Nothingness* §7 (read) gives the argument
as a thought experiment: imagine finitely many objects vanishing in sequence
until none remain. Its modal step is "What can be done temporally can be done
modally." It gives no argument for the contingency of each object beyond that
temporal analogy (this is an analyst gloss). The critiques reported there
target completability (Maddy; infinite divisibility) and the modal step: "from
a metaphysically neutral perspective, the fact that it is possible for each
object to not exist seems compatible with it being necessary that at least one
object exists."

At `n`, the premise "`n` could fail to exist" is S2 instantiated at `n`, the
denial of `n`'s necessity. The temporal analogy offers no support for it at
`n`:
- no vanishing of `n` is observed, so the analogy is not an actual temporal
  absence of `n` (§2.1d);
- an *imagined* vanishing of `n` is a conceiving (§2.1b).

|---|---|
| Target | `NecessaryAt m w n` |
| Mechanism | S2 at `n`, then subtraction |
| Independent justification | at `n`, none beyond S2 itself; its temporal analogy reduces to (b) or (d) |
| Relevance | it would deny `n`'s necessity |
| Status | **fails criterion 3** of the substantive objection standard; it carries no weight at `n` beyond (b) and (d) (confirms `03e1f49f`) |

**(b) Conceivability.** Take conceivability in the distinctions reported in SEP
*Epistemology of Modality* §4.1 (read): prima facie versus ideal, positive
versus negative, primary versus secondary.

1. **The datum is not symmetric across all parties.**
   - Aquinas grants that `n`'s absence is conceivable in the relevant prima
     facie sense: the opposite of "God is" can be mentally admitted (ST I q.2
     a.1, *sed contra*, read).
   - A theist may claim that `n`'s necessary existence is likewise not ruled
     out a priori (the possibility premise of a modal ontological argument).
   - Hume denies that datum. *Dialogues* IX, as quoted by Van Cleve §2
     (read): "The words, therefore necessary existence have no meaning or,
     which is the same thing; none that is consistent." The Humean therefore
     does not grant C(□E n).
2. **The bridge bundle is inconsistent only with a frame condition, and it
   does not choose its culprit.** Consider the bundle
   {`StrongBridge` (#248: whatever is conceived at `w` is possible at `w`),
   C(□E n), C(¬E n), a Euclidean frame}. It is inconsistent: ◇□E n gives
   □E n by Euclideanness, which contradicts ◇¬E n (analyst derivation,
   requested as F1).
   - An inconsistent bundle requires rejecting at least one member. It does
     not say which. Restricting the bridge is one exit; denying C(□E n), the
     Humean exit, is equally available.
   - Euclideanness is not a route premise. NECESSARY_2 assumes no frame
     condition, and the corpus states Euclideanness separately (definition
     `6f7a4ccc`; result `d9db5ed8`). On a non-Euclidean frame such as S4 the
     bundle is satisfiable, and C(¬E n) with the bridge then gives ◇¬E n,
     which denies `n`'s necessity.
   - So point 2 gives the route nothing. The objection's fate rests on point
     3 and on the calibration argument below.
3. **An ideal conceiving at `n` would need what we lack.** An ideal positive
   conceiving of `n`'s absence must survive full understanding of what `n` is.
   We identify `n` only through its relation to effects (first, unmoved,
   simple). ST I q.2 a.1 (read) holds both that the opposite of "God is" can
   be mentally admitted, and that "God exists" is self-evident in itself
   "for the predicate is the same as the subject". It locates the gap in
   this: "because we do not know the essence of God, the proposition is not
   self-evident to us". The article's topic is self-evidence. Reading it as
   "a conceived absence does not show a possible absence" is an analyst
   extension, which takes "the predicate is the same as the subject" to make
   the opposite impossible.

The "ignorance of essence" reply has two halves.

- **Epistemic half (neutral).** A conceiving made without knowledge of the
  thing's nature may not track (secondary) possibility.
  - *Single-occurrence support.* Conceiving `n`'s absence is conceiving a
    scenario-without-`n`, the single-occurrence form (2), C(A & ¬B). SEP §4.1
    (read) reports a form-(2) case: "water might not contain hydrogen" is
    negatively and primarily conceivable for someone ignorant that water is
    H2O. Such negative conceivability "often derives from ignorance of the
    relevant facts", and on Chalmers's account (as reported) "water is not
    H2O" is primarily but not secondarily conceivable.
  - *Analyst bridge.* `n` is given only by description, so its absence is at
    least primarily conceivable. Whether it is secondarily conceivable
    depends on what `n` actually is, which is the point in dispute.
  - *Reliability limit.* Further support is the reliability limit on modal
    judgment outside a method's range, from van Inwagen as reported in SEP
    §3 (read as report).
  - *Arnauld's triangle.* One can perceive that a triangle is right-angled
    without perceiving the Pythagorean relation. Van Cleve §3 (read) reports
    Descartes's reply: this counterexample defeats only inferences from the
    double-occurrence form (1), C(A) & ¬C(B), not from form (2). It is
    therefore used here **only** against form-(1) conceivings, such as
    conceiving a populated scenario without conceiving `n`. It is **not**
    used against conceiving `n`'s absence itself.
- **Metaphysical half (not neutral).** "Predicate the same as subject" is
  B6's esse–essence identity. It defeats conceivability only *given*
  simplicity, and is not counted here.

**Calibration (analyst premise, empiricist).** This is an analyst premise,
not drawn from a source: the reliability of conceiving as a guide to secondary
possibility is established only where its verdicts can be checked against
independent modal evidence.
- For ordinary concreta such evidence exists: observed generation and
  corruption, that is, actual temporal absence, which is also the evidence
  PGP's channel 1 uses.
- For `n`, which by the fixed meanings has no matter and receives no esse, no
  such check exists.

The premise is contestable (a modal rationalist denies that calibration is
required). It has a **cross-route consequence**, recorded as a shared
dependency: it equally removes conceivability support for the contingency of
the universe as a whole, or of any foundational candidate, wherever another
route relies on it. Any route that leans on such conceivability is weakened by
the same premise that weakens the objection here.

A conceivable scenario lacking `n` need not lack a first being. It could
contain another first being, or be empty. Conceiving it is a form-(2)
conceiving, and is assessed only by the reply above, not dismissed.

Neither side is credited with conceivability-based warrant at `n`: the reply
removes the positive conceivability of `n`'s necessary existence as evidence by

**(c) Recombination and Hume's dictum.** Assessed with combinatorialism in §4.

**(d) Actual temporal absence.** This is a genuine datum type, not generalized
from other cases: if `n` began to exist (actually absent at an earlier time of
`w`), its possible absence follows on a tensed reading. No candidate `n` with
a beginning is on file. For a being existing at the first moment there is no
earlier absence. The C2 frame indexes existence by world, not by time. It is
recorded as a revision condition.

**(e) Counterintuitiveness.** That first entities would be necessary existents
is surprising ("gasp!" in the Vetter handout, §10). The surprise is prima facie
conceivability of their absence, so it has no separate weight beyond (b).

### 2.2 Candidate data for PGP at `n` (the symmetric question)

**(a) Actualist truthmaker demand.** "Possibly `n` is absent" needs an actual
ground. Pruss (read) motivates the power account this way: powers "are things
we arguably have direct experiential knowledge of, pace Hume, by ourselves
being capable of producing effects", and "while having modal force, they are
concrete". But the demand separates PGP only from **primitivism**:

- Essentialism has an actual ground, the essence.
- Combinatorialism has one too. Armstrong, as reported in SEP *Nothingness*
  §7 (read), holds that "the truthmakers for possibilities are actual objects".

So the demand is not a datum about `n` and does not discriminate PGP from those
accounts. The experiential motivation concerns powers we exercise in producing
effects, that is, change-powers. By §3 these do not reach subjectless
existence.

**(b) Powers-based modal epistemology.** We know many possibilities through
powers: Pruss as above, and Vetter's perceptual account as reported in SEP
*Epistemology of Modality* §6 (read). At `n` this method finds no power for
`n`'s absence. That is absence of evidence. To get "there is no such power"
one needs PGP's completeness claim. Epistemology alone gives agnosticism, not
necessity.

**(c) The verdict as a consequence of non-theist potentialism.** The Oxford
handout (§10) summarizes Vetter's chapter.
- Under "Possibilities of non-existence" it records Cameron's question and the
  consequence "Whatever entities existed at the beginning of the universe are
  necessary existents (gasp!)", which it derives from "the triviality thesis
  and the temporal asymmetry of potentiality/possibility".
- Under "Metaphysical possibility in time" it records "the very first objects
  have necessary existence" followed by "biting the bullet isn't so bad".

Whether that response is Vetter's own is not checked against the book. If it
is, the verdict follows from potentialism's general content and is accepted
without theistic motivation. That bears on the charge of question begging, not
on the case.

### 2.3 Result of test 1

There is no case-specific datum on either side. The prior finding (C2 note §5,
§13) is confirmed, with three refinements:

- **Subtraction** does not discriminate at `n` (it fails criterion 3).
- **Conceivability** is a substantive objection with a contested
  non-generalization reply. Its inconsistency argument (point 2) needs a frame
  condition the route does not assume, and leaves the Humean exit open.
- **PGP** has no case-directed argument, only its general theory, whose
  motivations do not discriminate at `n` (2.2).

The one genuine datum type (actual temporal absence) is not instantiated.

## 3. Test 2: the hybrid's reply

**What the cited text says (read).** ST I q.45 a.2 ad 2: "Creation is not
change, except according to a mode of understanding. For change means that the
same something should be different now from what it was previously." The
reason continues, "sometimes it is the same being only in potentiality, as in
substantial change, the subject of which is matter. But in creation, by which
the whole substance of a thing is produced", no such subject remains. Q.45 a.3,
body: "Creation places something in the thing created according to relation
only".

**What the same author also holds (read).**
- ST I q.9 a.2: "all creatures before they existed, were possible, not by any
  created power, since no creature is eternal, but by the divine power alone".
- ST I q.104 a.3 ad 3: annihilation "would not imply an action on God's part;
  but a mere cessation of His action".
- ST I q.104 a.3 ad 1: "Non-existence has no direct cause; for nothing is a
  cause except inasmuch as it has existence".

So in the very source the hybrid cites, "existence is not a change" coexists
with power-grounding of the possibilities of existence and of annihilation, by
active power over esse with no subject. **"Existence is not a change" is
therefore not by itself a boundary for power-grounding.** It is a boundary for
passive-potency grounding (channel 1) and for change-powers.

**Can the hybrid keep the First Way's act/potency commitments?** Yes:

- *POTENCY_1* (`CanBe ∧ ¬InAct`) is potency to an act. A cross-world absence
  is no such potency.
- *Motion as reduction from potency to act* (prima via base `7bce7007`): this
  concerns actual changes. Creation and annihilation are not motions (q.45 a.2
  ad 2).
- *A thing in potency is actualized only by something in act*: this principle
  governs reductions to act. `n`'s non-existence is not the actualization of
  any potency (q.104 a.3 ad 1).
- *Pure act with contingency.* This is no conflict provided the hybrid reads
  `n`'s possible absence as bare modal absence, not POSSIBLE_NOT_TO_BE_1. At
  esse, POTENCY_1 contradicts actual existence (#1870's R2 law). The model
  `bruteSimple` makes every other premise of the PGP and C2 routes, including
  the prima via base, satisfiable with `n` contingent and POTENCY_1-pure act.

**What the hybrid must accept at generation and corruption.**
- By q.45 a.2 ad 2, substantial change is a change whose subject is matter.
  The hybrid that keeps the First Way's commitments must therefore accept that
  **actual** generation and corruption are power exercises: reductions of
  matter's potency by an agent in act.
- It must also accept that the typed PGP disjunction **holds** for every
  corruptible thing. It holds trivially, through its first disjunct
  `CanFailToBe`.

**What is not forced.** Neither commitment forces an explanatory power-ground
for a corruptible's *cross-world* de re contingency, for example that it might
never have been generated at all:
- The First Way's commitments concern actual changes.
- The typed PGP is a material disjunction, not a grounding claim.
- Never existing is not a change either.

So the hybrid's boundary ("powers ground what changes realize; cross-world
existence and absence get non-power modality") can apply even to generables'
cross-world absence.

**The exact point.** The hybrid's boundary is consistent with the First Way's
commitments everywhere except at actual changes. At actual generation and
corruption it must count the change as a power exercise. `n` (no matter,
nothing prior, no change) falls inside the boundary.

**Is the boundary principled?** Yes, as a boundary for passive potency and for
change-powers. Its independent motivations are that change requires a subject
(q.45 itself) and the Aristotelian notion of power as a principle of change
(Met. Θ.1, navigation only). It also **undercuts PGP's motivation**, though not
its verdict, at subjectless existence: the experiential and epistemic
motivations for powers (§2.2) concern change-powers. It does **not**, however,
supply the hybrid's own verdict at `n`:

- It explains why channel 1 is empty at `n`, which PGP's own verdict already
  uses.
- Channel 2's emptiness at `n` follows only with correlation and `b62a64ab`
  (§4). Whether `n` is then necessary (PGP) or contingent without a power
  ground (hybrid) is untouched by "existence is not a change".

The hybrid's verdict at `n` is inherited from its non-power component, so it
stands or falls with primitivism, combinatorialism or EC (§4).

| Objection from the hybrid to PGP at `n` | |
|---|---|
| Target | PGP at `n` |
| Mechanism | powers ground only possibilities of change; `n`'s existence or absence is no change |
| Independent justification | change requires a subject (q.45 a.2 ad 2); the Aristotelian notion of a power |
| Relevance | partial. It undercuts the **motivation** PGP borrows from change-powers at subjectless existence (compare §4, PGP's extra content). It does not refute the **verdict**: the justification restricts *change-power* grounding, not POWER_OVER_ESSE_1, and the step "no power grounds `n`'s absence, yet `n`'s absence is possible" needs the hybrid's non-power component |

**PGP against the hybrid.** The only consideration is disunity: one kind of
modal fact (an individual's possible non-existence) gets a power ground in
some cases and a non-power ground in others. PGP also splits by channel.
Disunity is a weak, matched consideration and is **not** counted as a
substantive objection.

## 4. Test 3: truthmakers and costs at matching scope

For each view: its positive motivation (independent of theism and atheism),
its verdict at `n`, the content that delivers the verdict, and whether that
content is independently motivated *at `n`*. The 542c595e-style test asks what
the view adds beyond what its motivating cases support, and whether anything
motivates that addition at `n`.

**Primitivism.**
- *Motivations:* reductive analyses of modality face counterexamples, and some
  basic notions must be primitive.
- *Verdict at `n`:* **none.** It permits either primitive fact and delivers
  neither.
- *Cost:* "possibly `n` is absent" has no actual ground, which matters if the
  actualist demand is accepted. Epistemic access at `n` runs only through
  conceivability, which is contested there (2.1b).
- *Assessment:* a framework in which absence *could* be basic, not a support
  for it.

**Combinatorialism.**
- *Motivations:* actualist reduction to actual elements; plenitude; Hume's
  dictum (HD).
- *Verdict at `n`:* absence possible, in the **unrestricted** form only. The
  restricted form is neutral (`03e1f49f`: the theist S5 model satisfies it
  together with the nodes).
- *Extra content at `n`:* certified difference `542c595e`.
- *Is HD the motivation? (Van Cleve, read.)*
  - §3 identifies two mistakes in Hume's use of the conceivability principle.
    Only one of them, conflating the single- and double-occurrence patterns,
    "is also involved in the methodological derivation of the Real
    Separability principle from the Mental Separability Principle" (§3,
    opening). The other, "moving illicitly from 'all possibly' to 'possibly
    all'", is shown in the uncaused-event argument (T 1.3.3.3) and in
    Baxter's reconstruction of the perception argument (§3, closing).
  - HD "can be accepted as a principle standing apart from them as intuitive
    in its own right. For why should anything ever depend, metaphysically
    speaking, on anything besides its own parts?" (§10).
  - A qualification exempting necessary beings may be added "if you like";
    Hume "would see no need for it", because of the conceivability argument
    of *Dialogues* IX, which Van Cleve quotes (§2).
- *Relevant difference (restricted to trivial co-necessitation).* If `n`
  exists necessarily, every `x` "necessitates" `n` in the trivial way:
  □(E x → E n) holds because □E n. That trivial co-necessitation is not
  dependence of `x` on `n`. For this connection, HD's dependence intuition
  does not extend to excluding `n`. The exclusion of a necessary `n` as such
  is supported only through Hume's conceivability argument, which is
  contested at `n` (2.1b).
- *Open consideration against the route.* The route does assert
  **non-trivial** dependence of contingents on a first being: B3 and CAUSE_2
  of esse; q.9 a.2's "by the divine power alone"; and PGP plus a first cause
  as the ground of creatures' possibilities. Van Cleve's §10 summary also
  records a strengthening of HD on which a thing "cannot require the existence
  of something or other beyond itself" (§4, not read). On that strengthening,
  HD's dependence intuition bears on the route's dependence premises, for
  example B2 `8a8d00e9` (every being contingent at `w` has a distinct CAUSE_2
  of its esse; #1870's reading of q.3 a.4). This is recorded as an **open consideration** against
  those premises. It is not assessed here, and it is not an objection to `n`'s
  necessity.
- *Assessment:* unrestricted recombination's extra content at `n` is not
  independently motivated: HD's core does not reach it (for trivial
  co-necessitation), and its remaining support is conceivability (confirms
  `03e1f49f`; revision condition (b) there is still unmet). Not examined:
  necessity of origin (Van Cleve §9, not read).

**Existence-conditional essentialism (EC).**
- *Motivations:* essence-first accounts of necessity; the thought that what a
  thing is never includes that it is.
- *Verdict at `n`:* absence possible, by default.
- *A cost carried over from SEP (analyst step).*
  - SEP *Essential vs. Accidental Properties* §1.1 (read) criticizes the
    existence-conditioned **characterization of modal essentiality**
    (modal-first). It "makes existence into a modally essential property of
    each object that exists" and so "rules out the intuitively compelling view
    that existence is modally essential to numbers (or God) but only modally
    accidental to Emma".
  - Fine's parallel objection, reported in SEP **§3** (read), targets the
    existence-conditioned version of the modal account of whatness essence:
    "existence is intuitively not a whatness essential property of every
    entity".
  - EC here is an essence-first theory of necessity, so carrying either
    criticism over is an analyst step. The criticism SEP names concerns
    necessary *numbers*, and it rests on an intuitive judgment.
  - The appeal to the necessity of God in the quoted sentence is a
    conceivability-type intuition. Crediting it would conflict with §2.1b, so
    it is **not credited**.
  - Result: the cost bears on unrestricted EC only, and only as far as the
    necessity of abstracta is independently warranted.
- *The matching-scope rival: concreta-restricted EC.* It avoids that cost: it
  lets abstracta exist necessarily and restricts only concreta's essences to
  conditional content.
  - *Verdict at `n`:* `n` is a concretum, so absence possible by default.
  - *Extra content at `n`:* that a concretum's essence can never include its
    existence, while an abstractum's can.
  - *Motivations for the cut:*
    - Conceivability of concreta's absence: contested at `n` (2.1b).
    - The general thesis that existence never belongs to an essence (a
      Kant-type thesis, navigation only): if it held, it would not respect the
      concrete/abstract cut.
    - Considerations from essences we understand without knowing whether they
      exist (*De ente* c.4, navigation only): these do not reach `n`, whose
      essence we do not understand (q.2 a.1).
  - **No independent motivation for the cut at `n` is identified.** This is
    the same status as PGP's extra content (below).
- *The crux, not a cost.* With simplicity, EC makes the route's esse = essence
  `78246da5` modally idle: the identity would not put existence into `n`'s
  essence (B6 `96971e31`; its brute-first model already witnesses esse =
  essence with contingency). That counts as a cost only if B6 is presupposed,
  so it is recorded as the **statement of the crux**, not as a cost of EC.

**Unconditional essentialism.**
- *Motivations:* Fine's essence-first account of necessity, endorsed by Lowe
  (2012) among others, as reported in SEP *Essential vs. Accidental
  Properties* §5 (read as report); essence-based modal epistemology, with
  Lowe's "Knowing an entity's essence is simply knowing what that entity is"
  as reported in SEP *Epistemology of Modality* §5 (read as report).
- *Verdict at `n`:* necessary, **if** esse = essence counts as existence
  belonging to the essence.
- *Cost:* that bridge (B6). Its epistemic route to `n` is only through the
  derivation of simplicity, since `n`'s essence is not known directly.
  Lowe's argument as reported in SEP *Nothingness* §7 (read) concludes that
  "there necessarily exists at least one contingent being", "even if there
  are no necessary beings". It does not by itself give a necessary concretum;
  the explanatory-dependence extension remains unassessed (`03e1f49f` next
  test 2).
- *Assessment:* a second route to the same verdict. It shares simplicity and
  so is correlated with B6, but it does **not** use PGP.

**PGP.**
- *Motivations:* actualist grounding; experiential access to powers;
  concreteness (Pruss, read); unity for change and existence.
- *Verdict at `n`:* necessary, given passive→receptive, correlation and
  no-self-esse-cause (all open).
- *Extra content at `n`: the 542c595e-style test applied to PGP.*
  - Because PGP's first disjunct is `CanFailToBe`, PGP holds trivially for
    every corruptible.
  - Its whole substantive content is the **completeness over incorruptibles**:
    every contingent being that cannot fail to be in substance is under some
    power over its esse (equivalence with excluded middle, or decidable
    `CanFailToBe`; the left-to-right direction is constructive; analyst;
    requested as F2).
  - That is exactly the part the hybrid declines, and exactly the part that
    yields necessity at `n`.
- *Is that extra content independently motivated at `n`?* By this note's own
  findings, **no**:
  - actualist grounding does not separate PGP from EC or combinatorialism
    (2.2a);
  - powers-based epistemology gives only agnosticism at `n` (2.2b);
  - the experiential motivation concerns change-powers, which do not reach
    subjectless existence (§3);
  - unity is weak and matched (§3).
  - The sourced instances of power-grounded possibility of subjectless
    existence read here (q.9 a.2: all creatures, incorruptibles included,
    possible "by the divine power alone"; q.104 a.3: annihilation as
    cessation of the creator's action) presuppose a creator. Pruss's
    "combined account" derives a necessary first cause, but from the very
    completeness in question. So none supplies a theism-independent
    motivation for that completeness.
  - PGP's completeness over incorruptibles therefore has the **same status**
    as `542c595e` for combinatorialism and as the concrete/abstract cut for
    concreta-restricted EC: general-theory content with no motivation
    specific to `n`.
- *Cost elsewhere:* Pruss (read): the account "has trouble with global
  possibilities". The possibility that none of the actual contingent beings
  existed "cannot be grounded in the causal powers of any actual contingent
  being". Handout (§10): "super alien" properties.
- *Bearing of the global problem at `n`:* none directly. Pruss's own sketch
  (read) resolves it within the account by a necessary first cause, which at
  `n` is the route's verdict. Alien possibilities have no actual bearer,
  whereas `n`'s absence concerns an actual bearer. Pruss notes that "to some,
  of course, this will count as a reductio". On the mechanisms identified in
  this note, nothing shows that the first cause could be absent, so none of
  them makes it a reductio.

**Is PGP's "unexplained lack of any power for `n`'s absence" a real cost?**

1. **Under PGP, necessity is the default.** The handout gives Vetter's
   definition as "It is possible that p =df something has* an iterated
   potentiality for it to be the case that p" (not checked against the book).
   Its dual (an analyst step) makes it necessary that p iff nothing has* an
   iterated potentiality for not-p. No positive ground of necessity is
   needed.
2. **The lack is explained relative to the route's premises:**
   - no in-subject channel: if `n` could fail to be in substance,
     passive→receptive would make it existentially receptive, against
     unreceivedness;
   - no other holder of power over `n`'s esse: by correlation, another
     holder's power gives either that in-subject channel (excluded) or that
     the holder is a CAUSE_2 of `n`'s esse, which with B3 makes it prior to
     `n`, against firstness;
   - no self-directed power: correlation plus `b62a64ab`.

   The structure of correlation has a sourced motivation: "Non-existence has
   no direct cause; for nothing is a cause except inasmuch as it has
   existence" (q.104 a.3 ad 1, read; no claim record). A power whose
   exercise is a thing's non-existence is then either a potency in that
   thing or the withholding of a cause of its being. Pruss's sketch (read)
   uses a related premise: "x cannot start any chain of causes that can lead
   to actuality's not including x".
3. **The residual cost is real but specific.** Passive→receptive `16c760a3`,
   correlation `9163c423` and `b62a64ab` stay open. The omission models
   `bareCorruptible`, `selfAnnihilator` v1/v2 and `inertPowerHolder` have no
   defended instance.
4. **The defaults are matched.** Default contingency under combinatorialism
   and EC needs no positive ground either. A sufficient-reason demand for
   negative modal facts ("why is there no power for `n`'s absence?", "why
   does no essence include `n`'s existence?") applies to both defaults
   symmetrically.

So the "unexplained lack" adds **no cost beyond the open premises**
passive→receptive, correlation and `b62a64ab`. The question that remains is
the motivation of PGP's completeness over incorruptibles, which is a parity
question, not a cost.

### 11.1 Three grounding demands, only one of which decides the case

1. **G0 — reality constraint.** An assertion of *genuine metaphysical*
   possibility must answer to how actual reality is, not to linguistic
   consistency or an arbitrary drawing of possible-world arrows alone. Its
   truth may answer to powers, an actual nature's restrictions, a real
   law/structure, or a primitive modal fact if the last is independently
   defensible. The modest version says there are objective constraints;
   the stronger no-brute-facts version is an additional thesis. Its appeal
   is the observed difference between imagining a scenario and finding a
   real capacity or a nature compatible with it. This supports taking
   modal claims seriously, **not** the claim that every possible *absence*
   has an efficient cause.
2. **G1 — positive permission or source.** For every unrealized
   possibility, something actual *positively licenses or makes available*
   the alternative. Actual powers are clear candidate positive makers
   for events; an unrestricted essence-first account may instead
   characterize possibility by an **absence of essential exclusion**.
   Whether the latter counts as a positive maker is the live disagreement,
   not a settled implication of G0. A negative essential constraint can
   be reality-grounded without being a power to make something absent.
3. **G2 — existential exhaustiveness.** For an *actual* being's
   metaphysically possible absence, a suitable in-subject capacity to
   non-being or an actual being's POWER_OVER_ESSE_1 is required. This is
   precisely the operative content of PGP 20d5fecd, not a consequence
   established by G0 or G1. At the selected \(n\), both disjuncts are
   excluded by its nonmodal profile. With excluded middle, the existing
   result 1e6174ab derives not-P. Calling this G2 “a real ground must
   exist” would hide the central premise in a plausible phrase.

**New discriminating observation (source-led, not a new proof):** Pruss
himself distinguishes *necessitarian* actualist theories (restrictions
from actual essences, possibilities wherever their negations are not
necessary) from *possibilitarian* theories (positive possible-makers,
especially actual powers, with necessity defined by the absence of a
possible negation). He explicitly describes both as Aristotelian
approaches, and his own powers approach as the second. This defeats a
simple *exhaustiveness of interpretations* claim: “all possibilities
answer to real nature” does **not** determine whether actuality
generates possibilities or essence excludes impossibilities.
Pruss, “Two kinds of views of modality,” 6 May 2008, opening paragraphs:
https://alexanderpruss.blogspot.com/2008/05/three-kinds-of-views-of-modality.html
(read 2026-11-23). This observation does not endorse either account.

### 11.2 Aquinas: actuality is more than modal admission, but the bridge is open

**Source passages read or rechecked, 2026-11-23.** *De ente et essentia*
c.4, Corpus Thomisticum (Baur/Koch/Busa/Alarcón Latin witness,
https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/oee.html), distinguishes what a thing
is from its actual esse and argues that esse, when *not identical* to
essence, cannot be generated by the essence's own existence-producing
activity without priority to itself; a distinct source is required.
It expressly exempts the possible case whose quiddity is its own esse.
This is positive causal/explanatory work about **actual derivative esse**.
It does not say that a cross-world alternative must itself be a causal
event or prove B6 merely by naming identity.

ST I q.9 a.1–2 (https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1009.htm) infers
immutability from pure act and simplicity, then separates a creature's
potency in itself from mutability through another's power; even an angel
with no intrinsic capacity for non-being can depend on divine power.
ST I q.45 a.2 ad 2 (https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1045.htm)
states that creation is not change, which requires an already available
subject. ST I q.104 a.1, a.3 (https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1104.htm)
treats continued giving of esse and indirect annihilation by withdrawal,
not a positive operation producing non-being. **No destructive operation
is necessary to explain a dependent being's possible absence**. This
is the strongest answer from Aquinas to a purely change-based version
of the objection; the answer presupposes the *creature's* giver.

The classical **positive explanatory route** is therefore:

- Actual derivative beings manifest acts received rather than produced
  by their own already dependent actuality (the per se priority
  argument, distinct from modal necessity).
- Modal alternatives involving their existence can be traced to real
  recipients, producing powers, and, for a creature without in-subject
  corruptibility, the source's non-giving. This is an intelligible and
  fruitful account of those cases, not just a list of world labels.
- A foundational \(n\) has no prior existential recipient/giver
  relation and no intrinsic corruption; hence the ordinary grounds
  of non-being are inapplicable.
- **OPEN generalization:** all genuine non-occurrence alternatives,
  including an *uncaused* being's never having existed, require such a
  positive real ground (or a positively exclusionary essential nature
  whose application independently settles the case).
- Only with that additional generalization is P excluded. The first
  three steps do not entail not-P; *bruteSimple* and the separate
  existence-conditional models test the boundary structurally.

The genuine philosophical support for the generalization is an
actualist explanatory principle: a *real* possibility should not be
arbitrarily attached to a nature without any metaphysical difference
that allows it; powers and essences supply such differences in ordinary
cases. One may accept this as a serious, prima facie primitive of modal
inquiry. Nevertheless, extending it from **what a real bearer or
giver could do** to **the absence, in another world, of an uncaused
bearer** is additional positive content. Experience of changes supplies
no such case. The first-mover/pure-act result excludes becoming or
ceasing, not every non-temporal absence by itself.

### 11.3 Aristotle and Pruss: powers do not automatically cover absence

Aristotle *Metaphysics* Θ.1 (1046a) describes the strict power as a
principle of change; Θ.2 and Θ.5 (1046b–1048a) distinguish rational
powers, including contrary effects and choice, from a simple one-way
natural production. The strict sense is a strong basis for explaining
possible **changes**; its extrapolation to all possible existences,
including uncreated or never-instantiated existences, is a distinct
modal metaphysics. For translation-based orientation see
https://openscriptorium.com/read/aristotle/074-metaphysics-book-ix
(read 2026-11-23); no primary-source claim record is created here.

Pruss's actual-powers proposal can ground non-actual **positive effects**
without constructing an independently existing possible-world object.
But at the selected \(n\), a mere power not to *give* esse belongs to
a giver distinct from what receives it, whereas \(n\) is stipulated
unreceived and uncaused. Claiming \(n\)'s absence is *therefore* not
possible either invokes G2 or makes an essence-to-necessity bridge.
The independent Pruss sibling result 42da6b2d concludes that **some**
holder \(y\) is necessary under P1′ and its full bundle; it does not
identify \(y\) with \(n\). #1897 establishes that actual-absence
PowerFor to POWER_OVER_ESSE_1 needs both a collective/distributive
and a power-sense transfer, so its premise is not independent
confirmation of G2. A creator reading supplies a positive maker
for *creatures* but cannot be imported to the first being as
a premise that the first being creates itself.

### 11.4 Essentialist rival and identity: neither is a free modal verdict

**Best standing rival to G2.** An actual nature can restrict genuine
possibilities without providing a *power for every non-occurrence*.
On existence-conditional essentialism, a nature says what \(x\) must
be *if \(x\) exists*. A genuinely positive theory of which absences
are admissible must supplement this with admissibility/plenitude
conditions, not merely assert P. This is a real explanatory alternative
to Pruss's powers-first account, with a cost:
at the specific esse-identical \(n\), the conditional status of
existence still needs a non-question-begging reason; ordinary
generation and corruption do not supply it.

**Response from act-essence identity.** If \(n\)'s essence genuinely
is its *act of being*, a conditional whose antecedent supplies that
act seems an incomplete answer to what that nature *is*.
Aquinas's *De ente* exception and ST I q.2 a.1/q.3 a.4 motivate taking
that identity seriously as more than a complex quiddity to which
existence might be added. This is a **candidate positive reason**
to prefer an intrinsically existential essence to a merely conditional
characterization. It neither proves identity is metaphysically realized
nor proves that the identity fixes the accessible-world extension.
The decisive open claim is AN 823d4312: an identical esse is
*exercised in the nature* unconditionally. Given the separately
declared act–fact link 3a2af70f, #1896 showed AN equivalent to bridge
565ccd6c; repeating it as “it lies in the nature” would be a circular
replacement, **not** an independent route to not-P.

**Best reply from EC.** A nature may be had and characterized only
at worlds where its bearer is instantiated; an actual act-essence
can be actual at \(w\) and never instantiated at \(v\), without
undergoing a loss or change. The existing *conditionalIdentity* model
shows structural satisfiability of this reading even with
represented esse = essence and an act–fact link. Its metaphysical
admissibility under **full** simplicity, underived actuality and
the intended theory of act remains unproved. The reply does,
however, identify an explanatory mechanism, not just an
unsupported assertion: absence is permitted by a negative
essential constraint plus a separate admissibility account.
Whether an act-essence can have this conditional form is precisely
the #1896 unresolved test.

### 11.7 Overall provisional judgment and next discriminating tests

**Provisional assessment (same scope, same date): parity at \(n\)
is retained.** Reality-constrained modal inquiry (G0) has a
reasoned, prima facie positive motivation: conceptual coherence
is not identical with genuine possibility, and ordinary
counterfactuals are accountable to real powers/natures.
The more specific claim G2 has a serious, coherent Aristotelian
and Thomist warrant for *dependent* beings. But the source-led
explanation of created possibility and the intuitive
anti-arbitrariness consideration do not yet prove that
*all* possibilities of a subjectless first being's absence
require a present power or reception. The strongest actualist
essentialist rival survives on an open conditional act-essence
and positive admissibility premise. We therefore do **not**
upgrade P or not-P, establish PGP, count G0 as a new root-route
premise, or assert that the surviving rival wins.

Next tests, ordered by capacity to discriminate rather than
generate synonymous implications:

1. **Actualist dilemma at the same case.** State a *nonmodal*
   and non-creator-dependent reason why an act-essence cannot
   have the EC conditional form. Compare a hyperintensional
   treatment of AN with EC without substituting extensional
   equality in an essence operator by fiat. Test the strongest
   candidate with the existing conditionalIdentity model.
2. **Positive-maker completeness.** Defend an explanatory
   principle that rules out *both* non-power essential
   permissibility and brute modal facts for *never-existed*
   concreta, while preserving principled alien possibility
   where warranted. Its criterion must not literally say
   “every possible absence has a power”; that is G2.
   Opposing test: exhibit a genuinely motivated actuality-
   constrained EC account for the same \(n\).
3. **Sustaining versus non-occurrence.** Check whether
   a subjectless absence with no transition can be given
   non-causal truthmaking conditions in an actualist ontology,
   without confusing the source's *withdrawal of its giving*
   with a power to make itself fail to exist.
4. **Model restoration.** Strengthen the existing structural
   *conditionalIdentity* or *bruteSimple* interpretation with
   full simplicity, PureAct as actually intended, rigid
   identity, and underivedness. An inconsistent strengthened
   bundle defeats only that bundle; a coherent one is still
   not by itself a real-world metaphysical possibility.
