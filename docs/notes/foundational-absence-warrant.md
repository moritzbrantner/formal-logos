The rival accounts are fixed as analyst profiles (attribution deferred):

- **Primitivism.** Some modal facts about existence are basic and have no
  actual ground.
- **Combinatorialism.** Possibilities are recombinations of actual elements.
  Its *unrestricted* form frees the absence of every individual (the
  unrestricted free absence of `03e1f49f`; certified difference `542c595e`,
  whose extra content is exactly the exclusion of any concretum existing at
  every accessible world). Its *restricted* form frees only candidates that
  could be absent.
- **Existence-conditional essentialism.** Necessity is truth in virtue of
  essence, and essences yield only conditional truths, "necessarily, if `x`
  exists, then `x` is F". No existence is unconditionally necessary.
- **Unconditional essentialism.** Some essence can make an existence
  unconditionally necessary.
- **Hybrid.** PGP for beings with a subject of change (matter), plus one of the
  above for subjectless existence.

## 2. Test 1: evidence for the possibility itself

### 2.1 Candidate data for "`n` could be absent"

**(a) Subtraction (Baldwin).** In the reconstruction surveyed in SEP
*Nothingness* §7 (read), the contingency of each object is assumed, not argued.
The critiques there target completability (Maddy; infinite divisibility) and
the modal step: "from a metaphysically neutral perspective, the fact that it is
possible for each object to not exist seems compatible with it being necessary
that at least one object exists." At `n`, the subtraction premise S2 *is* the
denial of the node (`6b12525b`).

| Objection to `n`'s necessity from subtraction | |
|---|---|
| Target | `NecessaryAt m w n` (the stage-one node at `n`) |
| Mechanism | S2 at `n`, then subtraction |
| Independent justification | none at `n` beyond S2 itself |
| Relevance | it would contradict the node |
| Status | **fails criterion 3** of the substantive objection standard; it carries no weight at `n` (confirms `03e1f49f`) |

**(b) Conceivability.** Take conceivability in the distinctions reported in SEP
*Epistemology of Modality* §4.1 (read): prima facie versus ideal, positive
versus negative.

1. **Both sides grant the prima facie datum.** The opposite of "God is" can be
   mentally admitted: ST I q.2 a.1, *sed contra* (read). The necessary
   existence of `n` is equally not ruled out a priori. That is the possibility
   premise of the modal ontological argument, whose escalation the corpus
   already has (`necessary-if-existent-symmetry-437`: on a Euclidean frame with
   the boxed conditional, possible existence gives necessary existence).
2. **The unrestricted bridge cannot take both.** The #248 `StrongBridge`
   ("whatever is conceived at `w` is possible at `w`") applied to both prima
   facie conceivings is inconsistent on a Euclidean frame. ◇□E(n) gives
   □E(n), which contradicts ◇¬E(n). This is an analyst derivation, requested
   as F1 in §8. So the bridge must be restricted, for example to ideal
   positive conceivability (Chalmers's weak modal rationalism, as reported in
   SEP §4.1).
3. **Ideal conceiving at `n` would need what we lack.** An ideal positive
   conceiving of `n`'s absence must survive full understanding of what `n` is.
   We identify `n` only through its relation to effects (first, unmoved,
   simple). ST I q.2 a.1 (read) holds both that the opposite of "God is" can
   be mentally admitted, and that "God exists" is self-evident in itself
   "for the predicate is the same as the subject"; it locates the gap in
   this: "because we do not know the essence of God, the proposition is not
   self-evident to us". The article's topic is self-evidence. Reading it as
   "a conceived absence does not show a possible absence" is an analyst
   extension, which takes "the predicate is the same as the subject" to make
   the opposite impossible.

The "ignorance of essence" reply has two halves.

- **Epistemic half (neutral).** Conceiving without the essence may not track
  possibility. Its independent support:
  - Arnauld's triangle case: one can conceive a right triangle without
    conceiving the Pythagorean relation, yet the relation is necessary.
    Reported, with Descartes's reply, by Van Cleve, §3 (read).
  - The reliability restriction on modal judgment outside a method's
    calibrated range: van Inwagen, as reported in SEP §3 (read).
- **Metaphysical half (not neutral).** "Predicate the same as subject" is
  B6's esse–essence identity. It defeats conceivability only *given*
  simplicity, and is not counted here.

**Specific relevant difference between ordinary concreta and `n`.** For
ordinary concreta, conceivability verdicts are calibrated against independent
modal evidence: observed generation and corruption, that is, actual temporal
absence. That is the same evidence PGP uses (channel 1). For `n`, which by the
fixed meanings has no matter and receives no esse, no such calibration exists.
The difference matters because the reliability of conceiving as a method is
established only within the range where its verdicts can be checked.

Van Cleve also identifies a *double-occurrence* fallacy in Hume (§3, read):
from "I can conceive A without conceiving B" one may not infer "I can conceive
A-without-B". Conceiving a populated scenario without conceiving `n` is not
conceiving a scenario that lacks `n`. The latter requires representing an
order with no first cause, that is, representing `n` as dispensable, which is
the crux itself.

|---|---|
| Target | `NecessaryAt m w n` |
| Mechanism | `n`'s absence is conceivable, hence possible |
| Independent justification | at `n`, none. The unrestricted bridge is inconsistent with the symmetric datum; the restricted bridge needs knowledge of the essence, which is the crux; the method's calibration does not reach `n` |
| Relevance | it would contradict the node |
| Status | **fails criterion 3** |

The same diagnosis removes the positive conceivability of `n`'s necessary
existence as evidence for the node. This slice credits neither side with
conceivability.

**(c) Recombination and Hume's dictum.** Assessed with combinatorialism in §4.

**(d) Actual temporal absence.** This is a genuine datum type, not generalized
from other cases: if `n` began to exist (actually absent at an earlier time of
`w`), its possible absence follows on a tensed reading. No candidate `n` with
a beginning is on file. For a being existing at the first moment there is no
earlier absence. The C2 frame indexes existence by world, not by time. It is
recorded as a revision condition.

**(e) Counterintuitiveness.** That first entities would be necessary existents
is surprising ("gasp!" in the Vetter handout, §10). The surprise is prima facie
conceivability of their absence, so it has no separate weight.

### 2.2 Candidate data for PGP at `n` (the symmetric question)

**(a) Actualist truthmaker demand.** "Possibly `n` is absent" needs an actual
ground. Pruss (read) motivates the power account this way: powers "are things
we arguably have direct experiential knowledge of, pace Hume, by ourselves
being capable of producing effects", and "while having modal force, they are
concrete". But the demand separates PGP only from **primitivism**:

- Essentialism has an actual ground, the essence.
- Combinatorialism has one too. Armstrong, as reported in SEP *Nothingness*
  §7 (read), holds that "the truthmakers for possibilities are actual objects".

So the demand is not a datum about `n` and does not discriminate PGP from those
accounts.

**(b) Powers-based modal epistemology.** We know many possibilities through
powers: Pruss as above, and Vetter's perceptual account as reported in SEP
*Epistemology of Modality* §6 (read). At `n` this method finds no power for
`n`'s absence. That is absence of evidence. To get "there is no such power"
one needs PGP's completeness claim. Epistemology alone gives agnosticism, not
necessity.

**(c) The verdict as a consequence of non-theist potentialism.** The Oxford
handout (§10) summarizes Vetter's chapter. Under "Possibilities of
non-existence" it records Cameron's question and the consequence "Whatever
entities existed at the beginning of the universe are necessary existents
(gasp!)", which it derives from "the triviality thesis and the temporal
asymmetry of potentiality/possibility". Under "Metaphysical possibility in
time" it records "the very first objects have necessary existence" followed by
"biting the bullet isn't so bad". Whether that response is Vetter's own is not
checked against the book. If it is, the verdict follows from potentialism's
general content and is accepted without theistic motivation. That bears on the
charge of question begging, not on the case.

## 3. Test 2: the hybrid's reply

**What the cited text says (read).** ST I q.45 a.2 ad 2: "Creation is not
change, except according to a mode of understanding. For change means that the
same something should be different now from what it was previously." The
reason continues, "sometimes it is the same being only in potentiality, as in
substantial change, the subject of which is matter. But in creation, by which
the whole substance of a thing is produced", no such subject remains. Q.45 a.3,
body: "Creation places something in the thing created according to relation
only".

**What the same author also holds (read).**
- ST I q.9 a.2: "all creatures before they existed, were possible, not by any
  created power, since no creature is eternal, but by the divine power alone".
- ST I q.104 a.3 ad 3: annihilation "would not imply an action on God's part;
  but a mere cessation of His action".
- ST I q.104 a.3 ad 1: "Non-existence has no direct cause; for nothing is a
  cause except inasmuch as it has existence".

So in the very source the hybrid cites, "existence is not a change" coexists
with power-grounding of the possibilities of existence and of annihilation, by
active power over esse with no subject. **"Existence is not a change" is
therefore not by itself a boundary for power-grounding.** It is a boundary for
passive-potency grounding (channel 1) and for change-powers.

**Can the hybrid keep the First Way's act/potency commitments?** Yes, at the
subjectless case:

- *POTENCY_1* (`CanBe ∧ ¬InAct`) is potency to an act. A subjectless absence
  is no such potency.
- *Motion as reduction from potency to act* (prima via base `7bce7007`):
  creation and annihilation are not motions (q.45 a.2 ad 2).
- *A thing in potency is actualized only by something in act*: this principle
  governs reductions to act. `n`'s non-existence is not the actualization of
  any potency (q.104 a.3 ad 1).
- *Pure act with contingency.* This is no conflict provided the hybrid reads
  `n`'s possible absence as bare modal absence, not POSSIBLE_NOT_TO_BE_1. The
  C2 batch already certifies that bare modal absence does not imply POTENCY_1
  at esse (law 10; `angelic`). The model `bruteSimple` makes every other
  premise of the PGP and C2 routes, including the prima via base, satisfiable
  with `n` contingent.

**Where the commitments do extend to existence: generation.** By q.45 a.2 ad 2,
substantial change is a change whose subject is matter. The coming-to-be of a
material substance is therefore a reduction of matter's potency to a
substantial form by an agent in act. The First Way's commitments thus extend to
the possibilities of existence and non-existence of every generable and
corruptible thing. A hybrid that keeps them must accept power-grounded
existence possibilities for all material substances: channel 1, plus the
agent's power, which is a change-power. It can restrict non-power modality only
to subjectless existence.

**The exact point.** The hybrid's boundary **holds at subjectless coming-to-be
and ceasing**, and `n` (no matter, nothing prior) falls there. It **fails at
generation and corruption**: a hybrid that put the possible non-existence of a
material substance outside power-grounding would contradict the
motion-as-reduction reading of substantial change.

**Is the boundary principled?** Yes, as a boundary for passive potency and for
change-powers. Its independent motivations are that change requires a subject
(q.45 itself) and the Aristotelian notion of power as a principle of change
(Met. Θ.1, navigation only). It does **not**, however, supply the hybrid's
verdict at `n`:

- It explains why channel 1 is empty at `n`, which PGP's own verdict already
  uses.
- Channel 2 is empty at `n` too, because nothing is prior and no
  self-directed power exists (§4). Whether `n` is then necessary (PGP) or
  primitively contingent (hybrid) is untouched by "existence is not a change".

The hybrid's verdict at `n` is inherited from its non-power component, so it
stands or falls with primitivism, combinatorialism or existence-conditional
essentialism (§4).

| Objection from the hybrid to PGP at `n` | |
|---|---|
| Target | PGP at `n` |
| Mechanism | powers ground only possibilities of change; `n`'s existence or absence is no change |
| Independent justification | change requires a subject (q.45 a.2 ad 2); the Aristotelian notion of a power |
| Relevance | it fails. The justification restricts *change-power* grounding, not POWER_OVER_ESSE_1. The step "no power grounds `n`'s absence, yet `n`'s absence is possible" needs the hybrid's non-power component, which the boundary does not supply |

**PGP against the hybrid.** The only consideration is disunity: one kind of
modal fact (an individual's possible non-existence) gets a power ground for
generables and a non-power ground for subjectless beings. PGP also splits by
channel. Disunity is a weak, matched consideration and is **not** counted as a
substantive objection.

## 4. Test 3: truthmakers and costs at matching scope

For each view, its positive motivation (independent of theism and atheism), its
verdict at `n`, and its cost at `n`.

**Primitivism.**
- *Motivations:* reductive analyses of modality face counterexamples, and some
  basic notions must be primitive.
- *Verdict at `n`:* **none.** It permits either primitive fact and delivers
  neither.
- *Cost:* "possibly `n` is absent" has no actual ground, which matters if the
  actualist demand is accepted. Epistemic access at `n` runs only through
  conceivability, which is non-discriminating there (2.1b).
- *Assessment:* a framework in which absence *could* be basic, not a support
  for it. It supplies no objection to PGP at `n`.

**Combinatorialism.**
- *Motivations:* actualist reduction to actual elements; plenitude; Hume's
  dictum (HD).
- *Verdict at `n`:* absence possible, in the **unrestricted** form only. The
  restricted form is neutral (`03e1f49f`: the theist S5 model satisfies it
  together with the nodes).
- *Cost:* its extra content at `n` (certified difference `542c595e`) needs
  independent motivation.
- *Is HD that motivation? (Van Cleve, read.)*
  - The derivation of HD from mental separability plus the conceivability
    principle involves two fallacies: single versus double occurrence, and
    "all possibly" to "possibly all" (§3, §10).
  - HD "can be accepted as a principle standing apart from them as intuitive
    in its own right. For why should anything ever depend, metaphysically
    speaking, on anything besides its own parts?" (§10).
  - A qualification exempting necessary beings may be added "if you like"; Hume
    "would see no need for it", because of the conceivability argument of
    *Dialogues* IX, which Van Cleve quotes (§2).
- *Relevant difference.* HD's free-standing intuition concerns
  **dependence**. If `n` exists necessarily, every `x` "necessitates" `n` only
  trivially (□(E x → E n) because □E n); `x` does not depend on `n` for that
  necessitation. The intuition's content is absent in exactly this case. So
  HD's standalone warrant does not extend to excluding `n`. The exclusion is
  supported only through Hume's conceivability argument, which fails at `n`
  (2.1b).
- *Assessment:* unrestricted recombination's verdict at `n` is not
  independently motivated. Relative to its case-based and HD-core motivations
  it begs the question there (confirms `03e1f49f`; revision condition (b)
  there is still unmet). Not examined: whether necessity of origin (Van Cleve
  §9, not read) would give a contingent `x` a genuine necessary connection to
  a necessary origin.

**Existence-conditional essentialism.**
- *Motivations:* essence-first accounts of necessity; the thought that what a
  thing is never includes that it is.
- *Verdict at `n`:* absence possible, by default.
- *Cost:* SEP *Essential vs. Accidental Properties* §1.1 (read) reports that
  the existence-conditioned characterization "makes existence into a modally
  essential property of each object that exists" and so "rules out the
  intuitively compelling view that existence is modally essential to numbers
  (or God) but only modally accidental to Emma". Fine's parallel objection is
  that "existence is intuitively not a whatness essential property of every
  entity". So the view cannot by itself *mark* the difference between
  necessary and contingent existents, and an essentialist who wants that
  difference needs unconditional essence-grounded necessity somewhere. With
  simplicity, it also makes the route's esse = essence `78246da5` modally
  idle: the identity would not put existence into `n`'s essence. That is the
  B6 crux.
- *Assessment:* **the strongest standing rival at `n`.** Its restriction is
  the only motivation identified that would deliver absence at `n` without
  conceivability. That motivation reaches `n` only through a general thesis
  that existence never belongs to what a thing is. The considerations drawn
  from essences we understand without knowing whether they exist (*De ente*
  c.4, navigation only) do not reach `n`, whose essence we do not understand
  (q.2 a.1). Whether a Kant-type thesis (existence is not a real predicate,
  navigation only) applies to an *identity* of esse and essence, rather than
  to conceptual containment, is **unassessed**.

**Unconditional essentialism.**
- *Motivations:* Fine's essence-first account of necessity, endorsed by Lowe
  (2012) among others, as reported in SEP *Essential vs. Accidental
  Properties* §5 (read as report); essence-based modal epistemology, with
  Lowe's "Knowing an entity's essence is simply knowing what that entity is"
  as reported in SEP *Epistemology of Modality* §5 (read as report).
- *Verdict at `n`:* necessary, **if** esse = essence counts as existence
  belonging to the essence.
- *Cost:* that bridge (B6). Its epistemic route to `n` is only through the
  derivation of simplicity, since `n`'s essence is not known directly.
  Lowe's argument as reported in SEP *Nothingness* §7 (read) concludes that
  "there necessarily exists at least one contingent being", "even if there
  are no necessary beings". It does not by itself give a necessary concretum;
  the explanatory-dependence extension remains unassessed (`03e1f49f` next
  test 2).
- *Assessment:* a second route to the same verdict. It shares simplicity and
  so is correlated with B6, but it does **not** use PGP.

**PGP.**
- *Motivations:* actualist grounding; experiential access to powers;
  concreteness (Pruss, read); unity for change and existence.
- *Verdict at `n`:* necessary, given passive→receptive, correlation and
  no-self-esse-cause (all open).
- *Cost at `n`:* see the three questions below.
- *Cost elsewhere:* Pruss (read): the account "has trouble with global
  possibilities". The possibility that none of the actual contingent beings
  existed "cannot be grounded in the causal powers of any actual contingent
  being". Handout (§10): "super alien" properties.
- *Assessment:* the global problem does not bear on `n`. Pruss's own sketch
  (read) resolves it within the account by a necessary first cause, which
  at `n` is the route's verdict. Alien possibilities have no actual bearer,
  whereas `n`'s absence concerns an actual bearer. Pruss notes that "to some,
  of course, this will count as a reductio". It counts as one only if there
  is evidence that the first cause could be absent, and test 1 found none at
  matching scope.

**Is PGP's "unexplained lack of any power for `n`'s absence" a real cost?**

1. **Under PGP, necessity is the default.** The handout gives Vetter's
   definition as "It is possible that p =df something has* an iterated
   potentiality for it to be the case that p" (not checked against the book).
   Its dual (an analyst step) makes it necessary that p iff nothing has* an
   iterated potentiality for not-p. No positive ground of necessity is
   needed.
2. **The lack is explained relative to the route's premises:**
   - no in-subject channel: if `n` could fail to be in substance,
     passive→receptive would make it existentially receptive, against
     unreceivedness;
   - no other holder of power over `n`'s esse: by correlation, another
     holder's power gives either that in-subject channel (excluded) or that
     the holder is a CAUSE_2 of `n`'s esse, which with B3 makes it prior to
     `n`, against firstness;
   - no self-directed power: correlation plus `b62a64ab`.

   The structure of correlation has a sourced motivation: "Non-existence has
   no direct cause; for nothing is a cause except inasmuch as it has
   existence" (q.104 a.3 ad 1, read; no claim record). A power whose
   exercise is a thing's non-existence is then either a potency in that
   thing or the withholding of a cause of its being. Pruss's sketch (read)
   uses a related premise: "x cannot start any chain of causes that can lead
   to actuality's not including x".
3. **The residual cost is real but specific.** Correlation and `b62a64ab` stay
   open. Their omission models (`selfAnnihilator` v1/v2, `inertPowerHolder`)
   have no defended instance.
4. **The defaults are matched.** Default contingency under combinatorialism
   and existence-conditional essentialism needs no positive ground either. A
   sufficient-reason demand for negative modal facts ("why is there no power
   for `n`'s absence?", "why does no essence include `n`'s existence?")
   applies to both defaults symmetrically.

So the "unexplained lack" adds **no cost beyond the open premises** correlation
and `b62a64ab`.

## 6. Provisional judgment (2026-11-23, unreviewed)

**Scope.** PGP `20d5fecd` and the denial of S2 at the candidate necessary being
(`b19ffd92` through `6b12525b`), at the fixed meanings of §1. Criteria: the
substantive objection standard, applied symmetrically, and explanatory
comparison at matching scope (does an account deliver its verdict at `n` from
content independently motivated *at `n`*?).

1. **Possible absence of `n`.** At matching scope it has **no discriminating
   positive warrant**. Its case-directed supports (subtraction,
   conceivability) and its theoretical deliverer (unrestricted recombination
   through HD) each fail at `n` for the reasons in §2.1 and §4. As an
   objection to the route's necessity it currently **fails criterion 3**.

## 7. Revision conditions (symmetric)

Toward "`n` could be absent", or against PGP at `n`:

- (a) A positive, ideal conceiving of `n`'s absence that does not presuppose
  ignorance of what `n` is.
- (b) An assessed defense that existence never belongs to an essence, applied
  to an identity of esse and essence (it would make existence-conditional
  essentialism a substantive objection at `n`).
- (c) A recombination or HD principle covering necessary concreta on grounds
  other than conceivability (`03e1f49f` (b)).
- (d) An independently supported case of an actual foundational being with a
  temporal beginning (§2.1d).
- (e) A defended instance of a self-directed or inert power over the esse of a
  being without matter (it would defeat correlation or `b62a64ab`).
- (f) A defeat of the route's simplicity.

Toward PGP, or against possible absence at `n`:

- (a) A defense of the actualist grounding demand that excludes essence-based
  grounds of absence (it would discriminate PGP from essentialism).
- (b) An argument that a positive conceiving of `n`'s absence is unavailable
  in principle, because `n` is given only relationally.
- (c) An assessment showing that the Kant-type thesis does not apply to the
  identity of esse and essence.
- (d) A defense of correlation and `b62a64ab` beyond q.104 a.3 ad 1.
- (e) Test 5 (Pruss) yielding a route to `n`'s necessity that does not pass
  through C2.

Global: a general-theory comparison that favours combinatorialism or
primitivism on independent data (for example, alien or global possibilities
that PGP cannot accommodate even with a first cause) would lower PGP's standing
at `n` too, since its verdict there rests on that theory.
