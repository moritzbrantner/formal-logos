## The precise adversarial position and conditional consequence

The relevant strong rival asserts **all four** commitments, at the same scope, rather than the bare absence of an ultimate source:

1. **No ultimate explanation of the actual total order.** The stance treats its observed law-governed history as a brute final fact, with no adequate ultimate why-this-order explanation. The closest existing ontic control is #737's `¬ GroundedTotality brute`, which is weaker still: *no ground of that totality in that particular stipulated `Scope`* does not assert universal inexplicability. In the formal argument this philosophical denial is a separately declared contextual commitment, **not** the cause of epistemic failure.
2. **Unrestricted relevant next futures.** Even after fixing a true observed record through time `n`, both an unchanged next event and its opposite are admitted as live continuations. A proposed total reversal of laws would instantiate this binary difference **only if** the reversal changes the selected measured outcome at `n+1` under the relevant retained initial and observational conditions; reversing laws without such an observable difference does not suffice. The formal surrogate neither proves reversals physically possible nor represents every law or metaphysical modality. This is the independent, testable `BothSuccessors` premise.
3. **An expressly endorsed strict norm.** When the stance itself calls a particular scientific prediction *entitled*, its standard requires that forecast to be right in **every** future it admits. When it calls a particular task policy *entitled to its advertised high payoff*, it requires at least payoff `2` in **every** admitted future. This is `strictNorm.Holds s.entitled`; no external critic may impose it on a fallibilist or Humean without argument.
4. **A positive confident projection.** The same stance does in fact call at least one of its specified scientific forecasts or high-payoff policies entitled: `∃ c, s.entitled c`. Confidence here is the normatively presented claim, not a person acting from habit or provisionally entertaining a forecast.

The **smallest incompatible subset** of the formal package is (2) + (3) + (4), with both outcomes represented on the **same observed record**. Claim (1) is **not used** to derive the contradiction. That is a substantive limit: no-ultimate-explanation alone is compatible with a stable local law and successful prediction in #737, and cannot silently be substituted for the admitted-opposite-futures premise. For any admitted `BothSuccessors` stance, `internalDiscipline` shows that satisfying one's own strict norm forces `∀ c, ¬ s.entitled c`; endorsing even one defined claim makes the joint package contradictory. This is a *failure of a position's own asserted entitlement*, not a proof that its metaphysical thesis is false. A stance may consistently withhold strict entitlement, employ a weaker norm, or supply a justified constraint that rules out the divergent continuation.

The norm's own *formulation* is not an object-level forecast in `Claim`; the formal self-application is of the stance's **own norm to its own positive forecast/decision claims**. It does not claim the more ambitious reflexive theorem that the norm is a proposition contradicting itself. The reusable #397 theorem `Norm.selfApplication` is applied precisely to each endorsed claim within the explicit scope.

## Universal deterministic forecast and policy test

The proof goes beyond the single #737 persistence forecast and the fixed-action `noOptimal` result by quantifying over **every** deterministic function `Trace → Nat → Bool` and **every** function `Trace → Nat → Action` on the existing action menu. At horizon `n`, each function sees the same observed `on` record and must give the same answer/act for both futures:

| Method's chosen output on the shared record | Admitted witness with opposite or poor outcome | Exact failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forecast `false` | `on`, which is true at `n+1` | Prediction false when outcome true |
| Forecast `true` | `delayedFailure (n+1)`, false at `n+1` | Prediction true when outcome false |
| Policy `.safe` | `on` (also the delayed-failure trace) | Payoff `1`, below promised `2` |
| Policy `.betTrue` | `delayedFailure (n+1)` | Payoff `0`, below promised `2` |
| Policy `.betFalse` | `on` | Payoff `0`, below promised `2` |

Both histories are actual defined `Trace` functions; #737's certified `Cell (openFuture n) on n` contains each for every `n`, including `0`. They agree through `n` inclusive and differ at `n+1`. This removes the empty-cell loophole and avoids defining error as a primitive or an opaque thesis. The proof selects a **countercontinuation dependent on the proposed method**; it does **not** construct one single future defeating every possible method simultaneously. No probability measure is attached to the admitted futures, so neither the binary 50/50 prior nor any statistical confidence limit is justified by this result. A randomizer would still require a declared probability/robustness semantics; it is not covered by this deterministic theorem.

The second half is scoped to #737's **three-action, ordinal payoff profile** (`safe=1`, correct bet `2`, incorrect bet `0`). It establishes inability to *guarantee the highest payoff*, not inability to perform a useful action, maximize expected utility under a warranted prior, or choose rationally. A real-world policy task with different actions, feasible fallback, goal and utility interpretation needs a separate instantiation; this theorem does not quantify over all human practical problems by changing the type of `Action` in the background.

Four theorem statements and proofs are drafted; kernel, Comparator, binding and NanoDa verification remains pending until the exact delivered revision is checked:

| `...NoFoundationPractice.internalDiscipline : InternalDiscipline` | Applies existing `Norm.selfApplication` under explicit scope and failure to derive the stance's withdrawal of its own entitled claims, or a contradiction if one is retained. | That any historical Humean, scientific naturalist, or all unexplained worlds endorse the strict norm. |

A prudent stance actually endorses the `.safe` policy under a *different, weaker* payoff-one norm; payoff one holds across **all** admitted futures, while its own safe claim fails the strict payoff-two norm. | That the fallback is morally obligatory or genuinely epistemically justified. |
| `...NoFoundationPractice.stableControl : StableControl` | The populated stable law propagates the true initial state by induction. The controls matter jointly. The strict norm is not a contradiction by itself (the stable positive stance satisfies it); the open future is not a contradiction by itself (the abstainer exists); and losing high-payoff certainty is not losing practical agency (the prudent safe policy exists). All interpretations share the same `Trace` and #737 `Action/payoff` semantics; none use a fresh explanatory or normative engine. `abstainingOpen`'s vacuous lack of entitlement is intentionally paired with the nonvacuous **positive** safe entitlement in `prudentOpen`. The positive stable model prevents mistaking the incompatibility for a trick built into every confident claim's type.

## Exact scope of the collapse

**The target is the expressly guarantee-demanding, future-unrestricted stance**, not naturalism or Hume's historical position generically. Its own confident assertion triggers a standard that its own admitted contrary continuation defeats. A Humean appeal to custom may explain an agent's expectation and conduct without endorsing certainty; *Enquiry* IV §2 explicitly distinguishes practical conduct from philosophical satisfaction about induction. #737 already compares brute-stable, fallibilist, dispositional, probabilistic and classical-theistic positive accounts with their actual commitments. None is eliminated here, nor does any automatically satisfy an ultimate why-question or independently justify a particular forecast. The issue is exactly whether the targeted rival **itself** endorses the norm and admits the contrary future, not whether a commentator would like to impose such a norm.

## Explanatory-selection and root-route connection

The relevant #736 result concerns *entailing selection of the complete history* from an identical admitted reason vector, whether the arena is source-free or rooted. #737's `Integration` passes those complete histories through a **lossy occupancy observation**: distinct total histories have the same observed presence stream and perfect local copy-law prediction. It then constructs a *different* open-future profile whose local evidence admits opposite next events. Our theorem consumes precisely the second branch and adds quantified arbitrary methods and a normative self-application. The #736 selection result **does not imply** `BothSuccessors` or the strict norm; an unresolved whole-history why-question may coexist with completely predictable local observations.

In the full adversarial package, the “no final explanation” assertion and the “possible total law reversal” assertion are distinct. If someone wants to infer the latter from the former, the exact open bridge is **absence of ultimate adequacy ⇒ law-contrary continuations remain epistemically live even after all actually relevant local evidence and lawful mechanisms are retained**. #737's brute-stable countercontrol defeats that inference under the current formal interpretations unless extra premises narrow its domain or rule out the stable witness. This is a dispute about adequacy of the interpretation, not an automatic logical contradiction in our world.

The second open bridge is **the rival actually adopts universal cross-continuation certainty as its criterion of scientific and practical entitlement**; Hume and fallibilist naturalists need not. A third, separate bridge would connect a necessary source and its actual law-application/access to better epistemic entitlement on a particular question. Do not identify ontic explanation, exact prediction and justified assertion. A fourth question for #1415's distinct global-explanation denial is whether a defense of that denial itself invokes an *ontic fact explanation* or only ordinary evidential reasons; this dossier proves no answer to that broader question.

Neither bridge is a current verified path to the Catholicism root `STMT-CATHOLICISM-TRUE`. This batch supplies a conditional **rival-facing test** for the #737/#735/#613 epistemic branch and names the missing root-facing warrant/application steps. It adds no conclusion that God exists or no worldview can reason, and does not remove/establish any root premise. Intended and actual corpus-root delta: **no change** in the root's premise set or closure (the authoritative computed `truth frontier-diff` remains to be run on the delivered revision). A local theorem about a stipulated norm is no endorsement of that norm.
