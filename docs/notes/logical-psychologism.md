# Logical psychologism: correctness and psychological realization

Continues the psychologism candidate in [#1989](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/formal-logos/pull/1989) and the consequence program [#1007](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/formal-logos/issues/1007). Research checkpoint, 2026-11-25; analyst formulations, attribution deferred. This is a linked research note, not a new truth-status authority.

## Fixed comparison and existing evidence

Hold the meanings, truth conditions and class of admissible valuations fixed. Distinguish an inference type's truth preservation from its occurrence, habitual acceptance, psychological realization and the subject's reason for drawing it. Do not infer a denial of noncontradiction from a theory about the ground of logic. F1 `c425f0c5` and thin F6 `f56b2ee7` are proposed destinations, not asserted new graph edges.

For a first classical propositional test, compare modus ponens (p, p → q therefore q) with affirming the consequent (q, p → q therefore p). In the valuation p=false, q=true, the latter's premises are true and its conclusion false. Suppose one population accepts both inferential habits universally. The habits have identical prevalence but different truth-preserving status. Conversely, two populations can vary in their dispositions toward one fixed inference without thereby changing its truth preservation. This excludes an exact criterion based solely on that prevalence, conditional on the shared semantics, not every psychological explanation of the ability to reason.

Reuse [identity](../../corpus/results/e03147fa-7a1f-48d9-bac0-7aec0ee2b645.json), [validity control](../../corpus/results/b203c461-805a-4572-b966-ba3a7886d561.json), [fallible correction](fallible-correction-931.md), [epistemic authority](epistemic-authority.md) and [rational basing](epistemic-underminer-boundaries.md#rational-basing-the-particular-transition-must-exercise-the-capacity). The first result concerns a semantic sequent class including identity, not universal assent or a comprehensive account of logic. The second witnesses validity and invalidity in one profile. Neither by itself supplies the metaphysical independence thesis.

## Discrimination of positive rival accounts

Baseline `a9819b148bd4a7c089cff9dc07f95c209452511e`. These are analyst variants, not attributed historical theses. | Account | Positive replacement and strongest response | Outcome of matched test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actual habit or frequency | Right inference is what the population actually accepts or regularly performs; the law may be statistical or deterministic. | A stipulated unanimous invalid transition and unanimous valid transition share acceptance. Even deterministic occurrence is not truth preservation. Frequency is neither necessary (unperformed valid inference) nor sufficient (performed invalid inference). |
| Counterfactual disposition | Right inference is what a competent reasoner would accept after reflection, with distractions removed. | Two stable policies can differ at the same countervaluation. If ideality means mere stability, unanimity, consistency of one belief set or absence of distraction, the failure persists. If ideality means correct answers in all relevant cases, truth-sensitive ideality, not disposition alone, does the discriminating work. This is not an objection to every fully specified psychological law: a richer input could distinguish the two procedures. |
| Actual practice or constitutive rule | The rule constitutes the content, or fixes the practice of giving and asking for reasons. | A different meaning or consequence relation changes the comparison, not the validity of an unchanged inference. Within a fixed practice, correct execution of its rule is distinct from the rule's truth preservation. A coherent rule-defined consequence relation is a live account, but an identification with truth preservation needs the semantic bridge. Logical pluralism is not thereby excluded. |
| Evolutionary/functional truth-directed rationality | Selection or learning builds a capacity with the function of obtaining accurate beliefs, and a malfunction can be widespread. | This has resources to distinguish normal performance from successful performance, and to explain why agents track validity. Local adaptive advantage or actual-world success alone cannot license universally truth-preserving inference. A semantic success condition can explain why the capacity is good without implying that correctness is a nonphysical substance. |
| Realization, identity or truth-sensitive naturalism | Contentful cognitive processes implement valid operations; perhaps a complete physical/functional account realizes the truth relation as well. | This survives the test: the psychological implementation can vary while the same interpreted rule stays valid. A theory that identifies or grounds semantic facts in a wider physical/functional base is not refuted by holding semantics fixed. It owes an account of content and truth across the relevant comparisons, not an independent nonphysical entity. |
| Eliminative global denial | No transition is genuinely correct, no matter what the contents and truth conditions are; all apparent warrant is merely an occurrence. | Denial of all semantic entailment in a class containing singleton identity is excluded by the existing theorem. Denial of all epistemic reasons is self-undermining and conflicts with F6. A mere causal report or a reasonless true denial is not thereby internally contradictory. |

The strongest live rival is the truth-directed realization/grounding account, not crude statistical psychologism. It can grant all local correctness judgments. The discriminating issue becomes whether the supplied semantic/normative relations genuinely reduce to the proposed natural facts or merely accompany them. The equal-frequency test establishes underdetermination by a projection; it cannot establish ontological irreducibility, conceptual independence of every psychological fact, or the modal possibility of minds with the same *total* psychological facts and different validity.

### Why a change of dispositions is not a change of validity

The fixed inference is an ordered premise set and a conclusion, with its interpretation kept fixed. On those inputs, `Entails S Γ q` does not take population or psychological states as arguments. Invariance in that representation is therefore a definitional feature, not a theorem about the possibility of psychology-independent abstract objects. The nontrivial comparison is whether a rival's proposed data can recover the correct classification of two inferences in that very interpretation, not whether a rigidly defined slot stays rigid. If the rival says contents or truth conditions change with the population, require a translation preserving content before calling the judgment a changed validity. A world in which no one performs MP is not needed as a metaphysical possibility: the finite control only omits its acceptance while preserving semantic input.

### Empirical self-application and the regress boundary

Let H be the claim that an empirical pattern of acceptance is the sufficient standard of logical validity. Let a researcher infer H from observed data D using inference R. Distinguish (i) D caused acceptance of H, (ii) R was the rule normally used, (iii) D actually supports H through R, and (iv) the researcher has warrant to conclude H. A causal or sociological explanation of (i)-(ii) does not supply (iii). An empirical survey might support a generalization about actual acceptance, but this narrower conclusion is not H. The inference from observations to unrestricted validity needs both a generalization principle and a semantic bridge. Merely applying the favored rule to certify that it is favored does not provide the missing discrimination against a stable, differently favored rule.

The objection is not that every reasoning procedure must be proved by a prior argument. A primitive rational grasp, reliable externalist entitlement or a rule-guided capacity can be a starting point; a complete survey can also be evidence about the populations surveyed. The objection is to a sufficient criterion failing its own proposed truth-directed task, and to H borrowing a notion of epistemic support it claims to eliminate. If its advocate offers no reasoned defense of H, H might still be true; that concession loses the status of an epistemically warranted rival, not its logical consistency. If the advocate keeps real support but identifies it with a truth-sensitive functional role, the rival is no longer an elimination of correctness. A psychological explanation of how a reasoner discovered a valid argument is perfectly compatible with the argument's validity.

### Evolution, ideal competence and objective success

Under fixed classical semantics, restrict observed trials to p=true (so q is true whenever the conditional premise is true) and the two rules need not exhibit a wrong conclusion on any eligible trial. More directly, a training set containing only p=q=true makes both rules succeed when their premises are true. Add p=false, q=true: AC now has a counterexample; MP has no true premise pair there. Repeated success, fitness or convergence on the initial distribution cannot differentiate the policies as universally valid. The missing condition is coverage of truth-preservation failures, or a proof from content that extends beyond training data. This is a deduction-specific application of #931 and the already registered benchmark/responsiveness distinction; it is not a new evolutionary debunking result.

An account that makes accuracy or fitting attitudes constitutive of the function can classify a prevalent error as a malfunction. It thereby preserves a contrast between a token's happening and its meeting a standard. The contrast does not logically entail a separate realm of logical facts, a categorical duty for a person indifferent to truth, or an intelligent ground of the standard. The remaining positive challenge is to give a non-question-begging reduction of truth-preserving validity, or a reason that no such reduction can work; the present finite comparison does neither for arbitrary reductions. Grant the same status to a realist capacity account and a naturalized truth-directed capacity account on the fixed examples. Selection history alone is not a defeater of either.

## Exact root destinations and denial consequences

**F1** is not the proposition that all logical correctness is irreducible. The exact record [c425f0c5](../../corpus/statements/case-non-contradiction.json) binds `CasePredicateNonContradiction`: for all sorts of beings/respects and predicates P, for all x,r, not (P x r and not P x r). The rival recorded there is an actual same-predicate contradiction, not psychologism. A valid MP sequent is not a proof of this case-predicate claim in a rival semantics. The existing Lean encoding makes its denial impossible with Lean negation; it does not encode a paraconsistent negation. See the existing F1 record and D-L. A psychologistic theory could accept F1 and say human dispositions explain access to it. The useful conditional consequence is: if a psychological rule makes P(x,r) and its same-sense negation jointly correct as truth assertions, and correctness is factive in those asserted contents, it conflicts with F1. Absent that bridge, acceptance of both by everyone shows error or inconsistency of attitudes, not the truth of both. No new `alternative_to` relation to F1 is justified.

**F6** [f56b2ee7](../../corpus/statements/there-are-epistemic-reasons.json) binds `ThereAreEpistemicReasons Reason := ∃ a p, Reason a p`. Its exact denial is `EpistemicNihilism Reason := ∀ a p, ¬ Reason a p`. If the radical thesis excludes every instance of the same reason relation, its conflict with F6 is the existing [f6ExcludesNihilism](../../corpus/results/146b502b-e5fd-40cf-8a39-9ed2d45e59bb.json). The existing retorsion separately excludes a reason for its own denial; it leaves a reasonless denial satisfiable. Denying irreducibility is not equivalent to denying the existential: a goal-relative or truth-tracking reason can be genuine. A validity fact is not yet an agent's reason to believe a content. Even if an inference is valid, the premises may be false, unknown, unwarranted, ungrasped, or not actually used. None of these conditions is supplied by truth preservation alone.

For a constructive F6 application, use the short proof selected in #1989 and #1987: universal falsity cannot include True without contradiction; distinguish this from trying to derive False by applying the universal statement at False (which yields only ¬False). Ask whether one actual subject grasps the same content and valid transition, accepts the conclusion on surviving support, and has that support as a reason for believing it. A valid proof object by itself is not an actual investigator's uptake. namespace LogicalPsychologismProposal

open TruthCore.Logic.ReflectiveCoherence
open TruthCore.Logic.CorrectionCriterion
open TruthCore.Logic.EpistemicReasons

abbrev Valuation := Bool × Bool
abbrev Content := Valuation → Bool

def atomP : Content := fun w => w.1
def atomQ : Content := fun w => w.2
def impPQ : Content := fun w => (!w.1) || w.2

def sem : Semantics Valuation Content where
  trueAt := fun w p => p w = true
  neg := fun p w => !(p w)
  neg_true_iff := by
    intro w p
    change (!(p w) = true) ↔ ¬ (p w = true)
    cases p w <;> decide

inductive Rule | mp | ac deriving DecidableEq

def premises : Rule → Worldview Content
  | .mp => fun c => c = atomP ∨ c = impPQ
  | .ac => fun c => c = atomQ ∨ c = impPQ

def conclusion : Rule → Content
  | .mp => atomQ
  | .ac => atomP

def valid (i : Rule) : Prop := Entails sem (premises i) (conclusion i)

theorem mpValid : valid .mp := by
  intro w hw
  have hp : w.1 = true := hw atomP (Or.inl rfl)
  have hi : ((!w.1) || w.2) = true := hw impPQ (Or.inr rfl)
  change w.2 = true
  simpa [hp] using hi

theorem acInvalid : ¬ valid .ac := by
  intro h
  have hm : Models sem (premises .ac) (false, true) := by
    intro c hc
    change c = atomQ ∨ c = impPQ at hc
    rcases hc with rfl | rfl <;> rfl
  have hbad : false = true := h (false, true) hm
  exact Bool.false_ne_true hbad

-- A supplied psychological projection identifies the two rules.
-- E may contain richer data than one acceptance bit.
theorem equalDataNoCriterion {E : Type} (observe : Rule → E)
    (same : observe .mp = observe .ac) :
    ¬ AdmitsCriterion observe valid := by
  intro h
  have inv :=
    (TruthCore.Results.Logic.CorrectionCriterion.characterization.1
      Rule E observe valid).mp h
  exact acInvalid ((inv .mp .ac same).mp mpValid)

-- An acceptance-only projection can fail even if everybody approves.
def allAccepted : Rule → Bool := fun _ => true
def noneAccepted : Rule → Bool := fun _ => false

theorem unanimityNoCriterion :
    ¬ AdmitsCriterion allAccepted valid :=
  equalDataNoCriterion allAccepted rfl

theorem omissionControl :
    allAccepted .mp ≠ noneAccepted .mp ∧ valid .mp ∧ ¬ valid .ac := by
  exact ⟨by decide, mpValid, acInvalid⟩

-- reduction or explanation is implied by this extensional classifier.
theorem richerProjectionControl :
    AdmitsCriterion (fun r : Rule => r) valid :=
  ⟨valid, fun _ => Iff.rfl⟩

## Executable finite comparison (run in this session)

This Python 3 standard-library check exhausts all four truth valuations, all four Boolean acceptance profiles, and all four Boolean classifiers on the acceptance bit for each profile. It confirms the countervaluation and tests equal-data impossibility versus restored discrimination. In the training-only control both rules really have true premises; there is no vacuous-success artifact. The final two one-agent controls simply vary the thin reason relation; they are structural surrogates, not a model of the full psychology of a reasoner.

```python
from itertools import product

rules = ('MP', 'AC')
valuations = tuple(product((False, True), repeat=2))

def evaluate(rule, p, q):
    implication = not p or q
    return (p and implication, q) if rule == 'MP' else (q and implication, p)

def failures(rule, worlds):
    return [w for w in worlds if evaluate(rule, *w) == (True, False)]

valid = {r: not failures(r, valuations) for r in rules}
assert valid == {'MP': True, 'AC': False}
assert failures('AC', valuations) == [(False, True)]
assert failures('MP', valuations) == []
assert all(not failures(r, [(True, True)]) for r in rules)
# Both rules actually have true premises in the restricted trial.
assert all(evaluate(r, True, True) == (True, True) for r in rules)
for flags in product((False, True), repeat=2):
    observed = dict(zip(rules, flags))
    classifiers = [dict(zip((False, True), outputs))
                   for outputs in product((False, True), repeat=2)]
    exact = [c for c in classifiers if all(c[observed[r]] == valid[r] for r in rules)]
    assert bool(exact) == (flags[0] != flags[1])
    print(f'acceptance {flags}: {len(exact)} exact Boolean classifiers')
# Thin F6 and a psychological acceptance relation can coexist or not.
for reason in (False, True):
    accepts = True
    f6 = reason  # one-agent, one-content domain
    nihilism = not reason
    assert accepts and (f6 != nihilism)
print('MP countervaluations:', failures('MP', valuations))
print('AC countervaluations:', failures('AC', valuations))
print('PASS: four valuations, four acceptance profiles, all four classifiers per profile; two F6 relation controls')
```

A definition of ideality as accepting exactly valid rules is only a coextension, not yet a reductive explanation. Resource bounds, stability, or internal acceptance alone do not imply the procedure's correctness. Test the calibration from the independently described procedure to contents. |
| Internal use versus content | The total inferential role might determine what a symbol means, rather than merely how tokens are manipulated. | Compare one complete syntactic response map under material-conditional and equivalence interpretations. Their truth-preserving inferential statuses differ; the rival needs an additional content-selection constraint. Full soundness of accepted rules can select a candidate connective, but cannot establish that those rules are valid for the ordinary material conditional. See the explicit positive control below. |
| Correct function versus incidental benefit | A faculty has a historically selected or currently organizational truth-guiding function, with failure conditions independent of comfort. | #943 already gives a positive mechanism of environmental discrimination. Grant it, then ask whether the relevant environment covers all semantic countercases and whether truth conditions are fixed by that mechanism. Successful action alone does not guarantee either. No universal objection to teleosemantics follows. |
| Attributive correctness versus reason-giving force | There is a fact about whether this is a good inference as an inference, but it has pro tanto force only for a subject with an epistemic aim. | Indifference cannot erase a countervaluation of a fixed argument. Nevertheless, whether that defect supplies a reason to this indifferent subject remains open under #930. If the rival offers a claim as deductively established to subjects pursuing truth, it needs soundness rather than approval even on its own instrumental account. It may consistently grant the latter and deny categorical force. |
| Realization versus reduction | Mental/physical processes implement semantic and normative relations; a wider relational base might also constitute them. | Realization accounts survive without explaining why the realized relation is correct. A reduction owes a determinate mapping and an explanatory case for its base; the constructions test specified projections, not the total natural facts. A reductive explanation that actually supplies such a mapping must be assessed on its merits. Nonreductive realization already abandons the stronger claim that psychology exhausts correctness. |

Do not attack a label by calling it circular. The same constraint can be
a legitimate primitive, a definition of a target, or an unearned assumption
in a *reduction of* that target. Which it is depends on the claimed
explanatory direction. Similarly, an account of how an inference occurs
can be both true and irrelevant to its type-validity, without being an
inadequate account of how it occurs.

### 2. Perfect psychological performance in a restricted domain

Fix a semantic interpretation S, premises Γ, conclusion c. Use the existing
`ReflectiveCoherence.Models` and `Entails`. Let C be the independently
specified subset of valuations a method can consider or has been tested on.
Define restricted truth preservation as the universal implication only
over members of C. Grant the rival the **actual truth labels**, not just
self-reports, of every member of C.

For any omitted valuation v in an expressive classical propositional
language, form a content q that is false exactly at v (the negation of
the conjunction of literals identifying v). Use a tautology t as the sole
premise. Then t entails q on C, but t does not entail q on the full
interpretation. If C is nonempty, the premise is actually true on every
tested valuation. This is not vacuous passing because no true premise
case occurred. In the two-atom model, all fourteen nonempty proper subsets
of the four valuations admit such a counterexample. Every singleton
premise/target pair of truth-functions was enumerated.

**General boundary.** For a selected inference class, exact classification
by restricted truth preservation is equivalent to the requirement that
each globally invalid inference has at least one countervaluation in C.
It need not include all valuations if the inference class cannot distinguish
them. If a tautology and an isolating false-only-at-v content for every
v are expressible and those sequents are included, coverage of every v is
necessary, as the construction proves, and sufficient directly by restriction.
Do not infer full coverage in an arbitrary infinite/non-separating language.
For the fixed two-atom Boolean semantics, the necessary expressivity is
provided by ordinary Boolean connectives, not an extra metaphysical premise.

This attacks a more generous psychological account than prevalence-only:
even complete, accurately labelled, perfectly consistent performance
inside a restricted range is insufficient for unrestricted deductive
validity. If the proposed ground instead includes a complete calibrated
truth-functional evaluation across all four cases, it succeeds in this
finite domain. No metaphysical claim about which worlds are possible is
made: these are semantic valuations. A rival who restricts validity to
a nomically allowed frame has changed the target; frame adequacy is a
substantive further question.

### 3. Can the internal functional role fix the logical content?

Grant a fixed complete response table for three **accepted-token flags**
p, q, c. The system adds q if p and c are accepted, adds p if q and c are
accepted, otherwise retains the accepted flags. The table covers all eight
input patterns; its operation is defined without a truth predicate. Grant
truthful connective observations in both diagonal valuations (p=q=0 and
p=q=1): c is true in each. The system performs both transitions and can
have correct outcomes in the actual matched training episode p=q=c=1.

For Boolean truth-functional meanings of c, there are sixteen candidate
truth tables. Write their entries in order 00,01,10,11.

| Interpretation of c | Table | MP sound | AC sound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Material conditional | 1,1,0,1 | yes | no |
| Equivalence | 1,0,0,1 | yes | yes |

The same entire response map and both diagonal truth labels fit both
interpretations as *descriptions of what the system does*. Even granting
MP's semantic soundness leaves precisely these two tables. The AC soundness
constraint then selects equivalence; a truthful off-diagonal calibration
c(0,1)=1 instead selects material implication and exposes AC's error. This
discriminating condition is not encoded by the eight input patterns.
The same syntactic moves can have different statuses under the two
interpretations. We do not say one and the same proposition has different
truth conditions. Nor have we held all psychological, external or physical
facts fixed: the complete data are just the stipulated acceptance-response
map plus the two semantic labels (and, for the strengthened fork, MP
soundness).

This yields a positive as well as a negative argument. A role account
that includes jointly sound introduction/elimination constraints may
successfully fix a connective. Here a soundness commitment about each
of the two elimination rules plus the diagonal conditions uniquely
selects equivalence. The positive proposal has a genuine burden:
explain why those constraints, not the equally executable but erroneous
material-conditional use, constitute the ordinary connective's content.
Inferring the soundness of actual use merely from a meaning chosen to
make it sound would discard the very possibility of error being tested.
Implicit definition, independent semantic calibration, environmental
content determination and primitive rule grasp remain different
positive options. The test does not claim their joint impossibility,
and does not subsume #1084 or the separate conventionalism research.

For an abstract deductive relation, the #943 account of stable guidance
across goals is relevant but not sufficient as stated: a system can
coordinate action correctly on every selected environmental task while
its inferential scope misses v. To extend the account to this case the
rival must provide actual discriminating tasks or a compositional semantic
argument covering the countervaluation. That is a concrete criterion
for its next explanatory improvement, not a demand that every premise
have an isolated proof.

### 4. Even near-perfect full-support reliability is not validity

For every integer n >= 4, use the distribution on 00,01,10,11 with
masses 1/n,1/n,1/n,(n-3)/n. Every valuation has positive mass. The
material-conditional AC rule has true premises precisely at 01 and
11; at 01 its conclusion is false. Conditional on true premises,
its failure probability is 1/(n-2). For any positive rational error
tolerance, choose n sufficiently large. Reliability can thus be
arbitrarily near one while the inferential type remains invalid.
There is no statistical inference from this constructed distribution
to actual populations.

This cannot be repaired merely by granting that selection or
learning favors success in typical conditions: the argument works
with full support and arbitrary positive reliability tolerance. An
idealized **exact** zero error-mass standard does work on a finite
full-support space: a nonempty countervaluation set necessarily has
positive mass. Zero *conditional* error is equivalent when true
premises have positive mass. If they do not, the conditional rate
is undefined (unconditional countervaluation mass is still zero).
Full-support, exact calibration and the type rather than token of
success are the nontrivial conditions of the positive reduction.
In infinite spaces a countervaluation can be measure zero, so do not
transfer the finite equivalence without an additional positive-mass
condition on definable counterexample sets.

This sharpens #931 without contradicting its positive fallible
guarantees. There is a difference between a rational, fallible basis
for a belief and a deduction. The reliable-invalid rule might provide
genuine probabilistic support for p after accepting q and the
conditional. It does not prove p. A proponent can sensibly claim the
weaker warrant; it cannot use that concession to identify reliability
with deductive validity.

### 5. The explanatory and self-application fork

A serious surviving account can supply a physical implementation of
a calibrated truth-function evaluator, then distinguish cognitive
failure from correct execution. In the finite case the computation
is extensional and explanatory at the level of how a competent
reasoner determines validity; no mysterious act is needed in this
computational description. It can be supported by a proof from the
compositional semantics. The issue is the further grounding claim:
why are these the relevant truth conditions and why does this semantic
relation make the inference correct? A causal derivation of a verdict
from its mechanism is not by itself an answer, even if the verdict
happens to be right.

If an advocate offers only restricted success, the counterexample in
section 2 directly defeats the claim to unrestricted deduction.
If the advocate offers independently calibrated full coverage, grant
the deduction and test the interpretation and its proposed natural
ground. If the advocate defines the in-scope truth labels by the
method's own verdict, distinguish correctness relative to that
definition from the intended fixed contents; the former need not
warrant a claim about the latter. A new calibration which genuinely
derives the intended labels from independently specified relations
would be a positive answer, not disqualified merely by being naturalistic.

If the thesis claims that its naturalistic ground is *deductively*
established by evidence E, there are two questions: whether the
psychological procedure actually yields the conclusion and whether
E entails it. The former does not imply the latter. Two candidate
grounds assigning the same likelihood to E receive no contrastive
support from E alone under the exact #934 Bayesian policy; unequal
priors or further evidence can still favor one. This is not an
argument that all evidence for naturalism is nondiscriminating.
If the advocate instead makes a defeasible inference, assess its
actual evidential support; the absence of deduction does not imply
absence of reasons. If it makes a mere autobiographical report,
it does not establish the metaphysical thesis for others.
If it argues for eliminating every genuine reason for belief,
the prior same-relation F6 retorsion applies. None of the intermediate
retreats logically entails that elimination.

No regress from the use of logical standards to a metaphysical
nonreduction theorem has been obtained. Epistemic priority (a
deductive defense employs standards) differs from grounding priority
(what makes those standards hold). Only an account requiring another
justificatory inference before every application would incur the
existing strong-prior regress; primitive rule grasp and competence
models do not require that. A purported reduction that merely
assumes the conclusion as its standard of ideality is inadequate
as a *reduction*, but a substantive reduction may presuppose logic
in the method by which the reduction is argued for. These are
different dependency relations.

### 6. Target implications and provisional judgment

F1's exact case-predicate scope and F6's thin reason relation remain
unchanged. The new comparisons give a stronger case against replacing
truth-preservation by frequency, nomic inevitability, bounded perfect
performance or arbitrarily high reliability, not an argument against
psychological realization as such. If any richer proposal supplies an
objective, content-sensitive standard, it preserves the relevant
correctness contrast, even if it attempts an ontological reduction.
If the proposal denies categorical force, #930's preference-independent
authority remains a separate obligation. No change in the agent's
aim can make an actual countervaluation cease to be a countervaluation
under the same interpretation, but it may be claimed to change the
agent's reasons for conforming.

The most promising next decisive proposition is more precise than
"psychologism is false":

> Can a completely specified descriptive functional base determine
> the truth conditions of the same abstract contents, including
> unencountered countervaluations, without using semantic correctness
> as an input or changing the contents to fit accepted use?

This is a **grounding** question, not whether a finite program can
compute correctly. Strongest naturalistic answer: compositional content
and external relations determine truth labels, from which actual rule
soundness follows; the labels need not be independent substances.
Strongest objection: the relevant abstract-content calibration and
countercase scope might not follow from the proposed base. Supply
one precise naturalistic calibration and test it against the actual
candidate functions above, rather than presupposing that all natural
facts are exhausted by internal dispositions. A verified exact
calibration that excludes the competing meaning on nonsemantic
grounds would weaken the stronger anti-reduction judgment.
A matched model of the *entire proposed base*, not just the response
projection, with a differing interpretation that respects its
content-selection constraints would strengthen it.

**Overall provisional assessment:** the descriptive-performance
versions are insufficient for the selected deductive target. A
truth-directed functional account that supplies genuinely calibrated
content remains viable; its explanatory burden is now localized
to the interpretation/scope relation and, separately, the reason-giving
status of that relation. This is a new scoped judgment about the
surviving accounts; it does not supersede the prior F1/F6 or #930
authority judgments, and does not establish ontological irreducibility.
No independent challenge has yet assessed this extension.
