# Can self-knowledge and self-love distinguish three persons?

## Proposed judgment

A single living subject can understand itself truly and affirm its own good. Even a perfect act, understood as lacking error, ignorance or frustration concerning its object, leaves open **who subsists as its expression and love**. The proposal of an inner Word and a Love proceeding within one divine life offers an intelligible account of how real distinction could concern *from whom* while leaving *what each is* numerically one. Under a personal interpretation of the two origins, their opposition distinguishes a source from each term. It takes a further reason to distinguish the terms from one another, and further principles to conclude that these persons exhaust the divine persons. The three-person account has positive explanatory value conditional on these bridges. Knowledge and love alone give insufficient reason to prefer it over one personal subject of the same acts.

The target is the begotten and consubstantial Son clause in `8a669d73-81d5-58b6-b7c8-0032859583f9` and the Spirit-from-the-Father clause in `e8fed8fe-9b2b-5e27-8970-0b8e8c9a1ec6`. The existing declarations are whole-article propositions in the Creed signature. A personal-origin witness earns candidacy for these clauses. Identifying this Word with Jesus Christ and this Love with the Spirit of the article, “begotten, not made,” and the articles' other predicates require further evidence. Even the metaphysical application to the actual God needs a common witness for the intellectual, volitional and personal interpretations. This analysis has no inference to either article or the Catholicism root. The Latin Filioque candidate has its own record. [Filioque candidate](../../corpus/candidates/creed-c381-filioque.json)

## What supplies distinction?

1. **Operation and term.** Understanding presents what is known as intelligible. Loving affirms a known good as worthy and present to the lover. A true account of what something is can be complete as a representation without yet affirming its goodness. An orientation towards it can depend for its object on how it is understood. These differences give the two roles determinate content; a second occurrence of the word “self” supplies no such distinction. Granting a complete internal expression and actual self-love still locates both in a single subject on the familiar case. Immanence licenses neither a separately made object nor a second subject. The prior corpus objection to treating the inner word as a produced individual makes that type difference explicit. [Prior inner-word audit](esse-root-leaves.md#f-recorded-objections-and-rivals-classified)
2. **From intentional term to subsisting relation.** The strongest case adds that a perfect source expresses its whole undivided nature internally, and that whatever intrinsic actuality is really there in an absolutely simple being has no distinct accidental act of existence. If an internal term is genuinely *from* the source and itself subsists in that one rational nature, its origin can distinguish a personal “who” without dividing the nature. A real internal origin does substantive work here. Simplicity excludes an additional accident if there is a real term. It also fits a single subsistent act which knows itself under its own intelligible form and loves the goodness already possessed. Perfection measures the adequacy of the expression to the thing expressed; adding another bearer is a further, ontological reading of adequacy. The single-subject alternative need posit neither an inferior internal likeness nor an accidental object added to God. Compare the existing argument for grounded self-knowledge by one's own form. [Same-source intellectual route](causal-perfection-transfer.md)
3. **Personal opposition.** On the personal reading the same person cannot originate itself *in the same origin*. Substitution now rules out source = Word and source = Love. Ordinary self-knowledge and self-love are reflexive in their object. Transferring that reflexivity, or the irreflexivity of efficient self-production, to *personal* origin on the basis of the common word “from” would beg the question. Here the positive warrant for opposition is the difference between the constitutive poles of giving and receiving the very same origin. Whether the real divine acts instantiate such poles is the lifting premise. A directed edge by itself only specifies an interpretation.
4. **Word and Love.** Two really opposed origins from the Father can still end in one person. One route holds that the Word is itself active in the origin of Love: the object as actually understood specifies what is loved, and love proceeds with respect to the expressed good. Converting that intelligible order into *personal* active origin is the further contested step. If it holds, the Word cannot also be the opposed recipient of that origin. The claim that Love proceeds from the Father, taken inclusively, leaves this additional participation open. A different route would individuate receptive persons by incompatible constitutive origins, generation by likeness and procession by inclination. Difference of explanatory kinds alone does not show that one and the same recipient cannot stand in both relations. This second route needs an account of why *constitutive* modes exclude co-reception here. Neither option requires silently putting the Latin wording into the Greek article. Neither proves that Father and Word are one spirating principle. A shared mode or terminus does not count principles.
5. **Why only three?** The proposal that the single essence is wholly comprehended and wholly loved by one indivisible operation in each regard gives a reason against an endless sequence of increasingly adequate representations. Applying this to *persons* requires more: all divine persons must be classified by unoriginatedness or these incoming modes; there must be one unoriginated personal bearer and only one personal recipient per mode. One adequate content can be entertained by several people; numerical unity of the common intellectual operation must explain why filiation individuates only one personal term. Likewise one loving act must explain the uniqueness of its personal term. A second generated person could otherwise bear precisely the same known nature. Conversely a shared active relation need not constitute an additional bearer: counting four relational predicates does not count four persons. The inventory classifies bearers. It does not exclude all additional edges, modes, origins from outside the selected act, or prove exactly two processions.

## Identity and simplicity

The profile ranges over arbitrary personal bearers, roles and act-of-being tokens. All three claimed persons have numerically the same token. This is stronger in its intended reading than mere membership in one species: three human beings can share humanity in kind and have numerically distinct acts of being. In a single-sorted reading on which each person is strictly identical by ordinary equality to one absolute individual, equality is transitive and the purported persons coincide. More generally, if the token map is injective, one token cannot have two distinct bearers. The formal result `8905b511-f4c7-414f-a4a2-fa531e71995f` proves the clash.

The many-to-one map represents the proposed reading of common esse. It supplies no independent metaphysical account of how the full essence subsists without division, parts, accidents or independently existing personal substances. At the personal level, real distinction by origin purports to answer why *this* bearer can receive an origin which *that* bearer gives, without a difference in absolute perfection. Divine simplicity constrains the answer; alone it does not select a theory of relation or a number of persons. The existing uniqueness proof quantifies over divine beings, and the created-intellect definition individuates its causal bearers by esse. Passing that very domain to the personal origin relation would invalidate the proposed reading. ## Revision conditions

The most discriminating next question concerns the *first* personal terminus. Holding the same simple being, self-intelligible content, and adequate apprehension fixed, what feature of a perfect expression requires a real *relation of origin in the term*, and which feature requires this relation to subsist as someone? A defense should explain what the one-subject, own-form account fails to express even with perfect likeness, without assuming personal origination as the meaning of “Word.” Once a first term survives this comparison, the next question is whether the order of known good to actual love is personal participation or only intentional specification. For exhaustiveness, an account grounding the unique personal term of each mode in the unity of operation would speak to the repeated-generation control. Evidence for any of these bridges can improve its standing separately; further finite assignments to the same unconstrained role names would leave the issue where it is.
