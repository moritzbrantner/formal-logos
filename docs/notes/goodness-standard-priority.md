# Does a good-directed nature require a prior standard?

## Question and fixed comparison

Owner-directed continuation of [draft #1962](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/formal-logos/pull/1962),
opened on main `1d84cb4b461b7a0aa8c1b6219b92556497c61bd7`.
The preceding reviewed [three-argument synthesis](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/formal-logos/blob/5c0c0f22c5b8bb74f0a36fc40b43d17c4087777e/docs/notes/optimal-creation-choice.md#three-arguments-about-one-productive-principle-2026-11-25)
is evidence from an unmerged draft, not a change already present on main.
This separate investigation does not duplicate that PR's files or formal
metadata correction.

**Question:** does an intrinsically good-directed impersonal nature depend
on an objective standard prior to itself, and would that dependency make
it inadequate as the ultimate source of the relevant order?

Retain the same necessary, intrinsically invariant, simple, concretely
productive source, nonnecessitating efficacy, objective recipient good,
intelligible order and genuine rational creatures. Compare the personal
and impersonal proposals at the same causal and explanatory scope. The
source's own apprehension and practical uptake remain disputed. No
conclusion against either full worldview is presupposed.

The investigation distinguishes priority in our concepts, epistemic
assessment, normative specification and real constitutive or existential
dependence. It tests an external standard, a standard constitutive of
the source's nature, and a standard constituted by the recipient's due
perfection. These are questions to investigate, not an asserted exhaustive
division of all accounts.

## Provisional finding on constitutive goodness

The existence of an objective standard does not by itself establish a
standard ontologically prior to the source. A source can be proposed as
good by its own nature, with the goods of its recipients constituted by
their own derived natures. That proposal need not make the source's
goodness depend on its approval, our recognition, or a separately existing
measure. The personal account needs the same possibility: apprehension
does not by itself make its object good.

This answers one objection, not the whole explanatory question. A real
standard of recipient perfection can explain **what counts as fulfillment**
without yet explaining **why this source's production is originally
directed to it**. The strongest impersonal proposal must place that
direction in the source's own productive constitution. Merely producing
beings with intrinsic standards leaves that further task unfinished.

The positive proposal developed below is a single intrinsically
recipient-perfecting nature whose actual manifestations supply the
recipient and the relations needed for its good. Its evaluative content
and productive relevance are substantive commitments, not conclusions
from a list of properties or successful outputs. The present investigation
finds no demonstrated need for an external standard in that account. It
also supplies no complete metaphysical realization of the necessary,
strictly simple, invariant, noncognitive, nonnecessitating source. The
standard-priority test therefore does not establish a comparative victory
over the strongest rival.

## Five priority claims and the inference at issue

Here a **standard** is determinate content distinguishing fulfillment,
deficiency and, where positively warranted, perfection in a fixed respect.
The word does not initially name a separate entity, an act of judgment,
an efficient cause or an authority issuing a command. A standard is
**objective** here when whether it is met is not fixed by whichever
verdict an actual assessor happens to endorse. This leaves its metaphysical
ground open; it does not define objectivity as independence from every
possible mind or from the source.

| Kind of priority | Claim in this investigation | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conceptual | Understanding a claim that n produces good D requires understanding D and the relevant sense of good. | An earlier existent, a producer of n, or a real constituent distinct from n. |
| Epistemic | We need grounds for the standard and its application in order to judge that n is good or good-directed. | That the grounds of our judgment are the grounds of n's existence or nature. |
| Normative explanatory | The recipient's nature and the positive perfective content of D explain why D benefits it, or why an exercise meets a good standard. | That this explanation is an efficient cause, an external legislator, or a complete explanation of production. |
| Ontic | Some reality is required to constitute, actualize or sustain n, its power, or its actual direction. | This stronger dependency cannot be inferred merely from conceptual intelligibility, warranted evaluation or normative relevance. |
| Constitutive | What n is, or what the recipient's power is, supplies the relevant standard as part of its nature. | A distinct superior standard, or n causing itself. Real identity, an internal constitutive relation and mere coextension remain different proposals. |

The constitution proposal does not say that an independently existing n
first creates its own essence and then makes that essence good. Nor need
it place n on both sides of an irreflexive grounding relation. It can say
that one actuality is the concrete source and has a positive perfective
character essentially. Statements about that actuality can be explained
under different descriptions without thereby being different components
or different causes. Whether this particular identity is true, and whether
the descriptions can be realized without forbidden composition, remain
substantive questions.

Likewise, **independently characterizing D** means that its meaning and
warrant are not read solely from H's occurrence. It does not mean that
the reality grounding D must be ontologically independent of n. Requiring
an independent criterion for assessment and then treating that criterion
as an independent constituent of reality would repeat the very inference
under examination.

An external normative fact could also be explanatorily prior to the
**predication** that n is good without creating or constituting n. If the
claimed scope is complete explanation of all evaluative truth, that
unaccounted-for fact matters to the claim of completeness. If the scope
is only ultimate concrete efficient causation, the same fact does not
automatically defeat it. One must identify the actual relation and the
scope of ultimacy before alleging a contradiction. The root-facing
definitions must not be strengthened by the ordinary word "ultimate."

## Hold positive good fixed, rather than weakening the rival

The selected D remains the positive good of a genuine finite knower
exercising its own understanding with appropriate access to correction.
An imposed correct response is insufficient: the recipient itself must
understand and judge. Its relevant perfection is positively good for that
recipient, not merely less bad than an alternative or useful to an
external evaluator. These are the comparison's granted conditions; the
argument must not reclassify the rival's rational creatures as simulations
or its positive goods as value-neutral outputs.

The [existing eye/repair comparison](root-esse-goodness-prd.md#does-a-warranted-judgment-of-improvement-settle-positive-goodness)
shows why this qualification matters. A fixed success criterion and
identical rankings of improvement do not determine whether fulfillment is
positive excellence or merely the absence of a defect. The present rival
grants the positive interpretation. That removes one disputed premise
from this comparison; it does not solve the original question of where
that evaluative character belongs in the ultimate explanation.

Three further distinctions survive that grant:

1. **A good in x is not yet intrinsic goodness in n.** A source can be
   useful to something without possessing the good formally or being
   morally good. The [source's own existential goodness investigation](root-esse-goodness-prd.md#51-direct-constitutive-actuality-route)
   retains a positive constitutive-actuality rationale and its evaluative
   premise; efficient provision alone does not replace that rationale.
2. **A standard in x is not yet original finality in n.** Giving a bearer
   a capacity can give it a determinate fulfillment condition without
   showing that fulfilling that capacity specifies the giver's operation.
3. **A good in one respect is not the goodness of every act or of a whole
   order.** The due actuality of a power does not settle conflicting goods,
   moral rightness, categorical obligation, or mandatory maximization.
   Those stronger questions retain their own subjects and premises.

The existing [`DueActualityThesis`](../../TruthCore/Metaethics/GoodnessFormEnd.lean)
is appropriately narrower: `GoodIn x p` coextends with actual possession
of a perfection due to a supplied form and end. The independent `GoodIn`
relation can fail to match that standard. The thesis encodes neither
grounding nor original final causation. Its [registered bridge](../../corpus/results/GOODNESS-DUE-ACTUALITY-WITH-DUE-BRIDGE-ENTAILS-UNRESTRICTED.json)
to unrestricted actuality additionally requires `AllActualitiesDue` in
the same frame. No use below silently identifies those propositions or
infers property identity from their extensions.

## What the primary goodness texts contribute

These are research consultations of the linked translations, not new
materialized passage witnesses or source-to-statement records. The
interpretive contribution is distinct from independent warrant for the
metaphysical premises.

In [ST I q.5 a.1](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1005.htm#article1),
Aquinas relates goodness to actuality through perfection and desirability,
while distinguishing the concepts. Article 2 gives being conceptual
priority but goodness priority in final causality. Article 4, reply 2,
interprets self-diffusion through an end's moving, rather than making an
abstract good another efficient agent. Article 5, reply 2, rejects a
regress in which each formal good-maker needs another good-maker of the
same kind. These passages support separating conceptual, formal and final
explanation. They do not establish the proposed noncognitive source or
make every fulfilled descriptive condition positively good.

In [ST I q.6 a.3](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1006.htm#article3),
essential goodness requires more than a fixed or necessary trait: Aquinas
appeals to perfection in being, operation and end, with essence/existence
identity, no accidents and no higher end. Reply 3 does not demand a further
goodness making each goodness good. Article 4 distinguishes the first
exemplary, efficient and final principle from the recipient's own formal
goodness. Articles 1–2 also appeal to likeness and causal communication;
mere causal usefulness is insufficient. This is a positive precedent for
intrinsic local goods with one ultimate source. It is not an attribution
of the impersonal proposal to Aquinas, who explicitly retains higher
intelligence in a.1, reply 2.

The relevant lesson for the proposed regress is precise. If a goodness
constitutes a bearer as good in a respect, it need not itself be a second
bearer awaiting another instance of goodness. If the first source is
essentially good, that is a substantive account of its constitution, not
an exception granted merely because it is called personal. Applying the
same distinction to a noncognitive candidate requires assessing the
candidate's proposed nature; excluding it needs a further argument about
that nature. The text's own divine identification does not independently
supply that exclusion.

## A positive intrinsic constitution at the matched scope

The strongest proposal has more content than "whatever n does is good."
The following is an analyst reconstruction, not a new formal definition
or a historical attribution.

**First, identify the recipient good without reading it from the output.**
For D, the bearer is the actual knower x. Relevant fulfillment includes
understanding a determinate subject matter, judging through relevant
reasons, and retaining access to correction under specified challenges.
The claim that such actualization positively perfects x is retained as
evaluative content. It can have direct and cumulative warrant from the
activity of understanding; it is not deduced from merely labeling a
state normal or successful. This content makes it possible to distinguish
a genuine exercise from a forced answer, an unsupported true guess or
a process which disables correction.

**Second, give the local standard a constitutive home.** The recipient
has its fulfillment conditions because of what its powers and their
objects are. On this account, n produces a real rational bearer rather
than first producing a normless mechanism and then importing an external
evaluator's verdict. The goodness is formally the recipient's own; its
concrete actuality and nature can nevertheless be derived from n. A
truth about what would fulfill such a nature need not be an additional
concrete entity waiting to be created before the nature can be produced.
This leaves open how necessary truths about possible natures are
grounded, rather than answering it by reifying every conditional standard.

**Third, place the original productive direction in n.** The proposal is
that n's one intrinsic actuality has a recipient-perfecting character:
its proper productive manifestation realizes D together with the
relations needed for D. Those relations include the knower's real access
to its subject matter and the relevance of its evidence, not merely
the coexistence of a thinker and objects. The source produces their fit
as part of the same manifestation. A source with no such orientation
would not satisfy the proposal merely because useful creatures happened
to result from it.

**Fourth, locate the evaluative content within that proposed nature.**
To answer the strongest full-ground objection, the rival cannot silently
add an independently subsisting axiology which supplies all of the
source's positive direction. It proposes that the perfective character
is intrinsic to the very actuality that is productive. Normative and
productive descriptions pick out one nature; an external norm does not
assemble or activate it. This is stronger than the claim that the
manifestations happen to coextend with an independently supplied good.
It is also stronger than a wholly descriptive capacity whose success
receives value only through another unexplained principle.

The proposal has a noncircular direction of explanation. The contents of
understanding, reasons, correction and positive fulfillment are fixed
before H is judged successful. The proposed nature then explains why
its actual exercise jointly supplies the relevant conditions. Neither
"H came from n" nor "n produced H" is the sole reason for calling H
good. The remaining foundational commitment is that this intrinsically
perfective productive nature is real and belongs to the actual source.
A primitive with that content is not a circular deduction; it remains a
primitive whose warrant, explanatory work and compatibility must be
assessed. The personal proposal has the same permission and burden.

This is an irreducibly evaluative constitution, not a deduction of value
from wholly nonevaluative premises. The word "intrinsic" does not license
it by itself. The benefit of the proposal is its direction and substantive
content: no antecedent endorsement fixes the good, and no separate
good-maker is introduced merely to make that good-maker good. Whether
this actually grounds every relevant normative fact, rather than leaving
an independent co-ground, is still part of its full-source application.

Nor does this give the source an acquired perfection when x succeeds.
The rival proposes the source's intrinsic actuality as already complete;
the recipient's fulfillment is the extrinsic term of its giving. The
source's lack of recipient cognition is not a defect merely by the
recipient's standard. To establish that cognition is also a perfection
the source must possess requires the relevant same-source
perfection-transfer or actual-intellect argument. It cannot be obtained
by transferring x's form/end table to n unchanged.

This construction is a substantive proposal, but it is not a certified
strictly simple realization. Different explanatory descriptions do not
prove composition; asserting that they describe one actuality does not
prove simplicity either. The underlying positive nature must actually
support the descriptions without received actualizers, independently
combined principles or a forbidden limitation. No table of freely
assigned `Good`, `Simple`, `Productive` and `Noncognitive` flags would
settle those questions.

## When that constitution would explain original finality

The [reviewed joint-specification finding in draft #1962](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/formal-logos/blob/5c0c0f22c5b8bb74f0a36fc40b43d17c4087777e/docs/notes/teleology-first-mover.md#the-strongest-rival-supplies-the-relations-together)
is reused at its stated scope. Let K name the relations constitutively
required for D. A manifestation realizing D must realize K; the relations
need not each be an unrelated primitive. This constrains a proposed
manifestation jointly. It does not establish a power, its simplicity,
an actual exercise, or the causal applicability of the description.

The further test is whether D helps individuate **this actual productive
exercise as a proper manifestation**. A purported explanation must
distinguish what fulfills its orientation from an incidental beneficial
effect. It must account for why K serves the recipient's positive good
in the same production. If it does so, the noncognitive nature supplies
formal/final explanatory content: D explains why these several relations
belong together in that manifestation. No second efficient agent is
required just because the explanation is normative or final.

By contrast, a merely productive source plus downstream goods supplies
only their origin. The local constitution of the recipient's standard
does not itself turn that origin into original good-directedness. The
strengthened rival addresses the gap by making recipient perfection the
proper end of the source's own operation. The live burden is that
**actual constitution and application**, not the general existence of
standards or an obligation to derive every primitive from an earlier one.

[De veritate q.5 a.2](https://isidore.co/aquinas/english/QDdeVer5.htm)
sharpens the target: the beneficial measure of an activity needs
explanation beyond that activity considered alone. The body then
requires cognitive direction; replies 4–5 and 10 locate original
ordering in divine knowledge and will, treating natural direction as
received. The strengthened rival grants the good measure and offers
intrinsic original direction. The text supplies the positive intellectual
account and the disputed exclusion, not an independent demonstration
against that stipulated replacement. This is the same boundary retained
in the prior reviewed continuation.

Standing orientation also does not establish occurrence or a complete
contrastive explanation of H. Necessary truth about what D requires is
different from necessary realization of D. If an account additionally
makes the source's necessary operation strictly necessitate a particular
recipient, that recipient's admissible absence is excluded. If it
necessitates only a recipient role, replacements may preserve it. These
modal consequences are exactly scoped in [#1962 §§55–58](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/formal-logos/blob/5c0c0f22c5b8bb74f0a36fc40b43d17c4087777e/docs/notes/optimal-creation-choice.md#55-where-an-impersonal-account-fails-and-how-the-strongest-one-answers).
This inquiry adds no new necessity or total-noncreation assumption.

## Concrete controls for the proposed inference

The following are analytic cases or reused restricted controls, not
observations establishing an ultimate source's metaphysical possibility.
Each targets a particular inference, rather than purporting to model the
entire retained source bundle.

| Control | What it tests | Limited outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The same eye loses and regains a specified discriminating capacity. | Whether a standard can concern the bearer's own power, and whether improvement alone establishes positive goodness. | It can be a local constitutive standard; the positive evaluative interpretation remains additional. This does not establish that the eye or its norm has no intelligent source. |
| A recipient gives a correct answer because another agent imposes it, while lacking the understanding required by D. | Whether successful-looking output is sufficient for the chosen good. | It is not a D-realization. The strong impersonal candidate must supply the recipient's actual understanding, just as the personal candidate must. |
| Correcting a judgment by reversing it helps when the original judgment is false and damages correctness when it is true. | Whether a fixed physical intervention can replace the relation to the recipient's cognitive fulfillment. | A physical proxy alone is insufficient. Both understood direction and intrinsic recipient-relative direction can respect the reversal; the reversal does not decide between them. |
| An agent understands an effect and deliberately uses it to frustrate the recipient's understanding. | Whether apprehension, intelligible choice or efficient production alone makes the object or activity good. | None does. The personal good-by-nature account needs its positive nature and right productive orientation, not merely cognition. No claim about the possibility of an evil perfect source follows. |
| A capacity is fully actual in one bearer but destructive to another. | Whether actuality in a respect or a source's causal role supplies an unrestricted moral verdict. | The bearer, perfection and good-for relation matter. Neither local due goodness nor source ownership alone settles the goodness of the whole order. |

A noncircular intrinsic account can pass the first three controls because
its standard is fixed by substantive recipient perfection, not by the
particular output or its creator's approval. Passing does not show that
n has that constitution. The last two controls prevent the personal
account from receiving the required good-making content merely by adding
apprehension. They also prevent intrinsic nature from becoming a universal
license to count any necessary or actual determination as good.

In particular, necessity is no substitute for the evaluative step. A
fixed specification for frustrating a creature's understanding does not
become perfective merely because it never changes. The noncognitive and
personal proposals must both identify why their particular content is
positively good, rather than merely immutable.

## Apply the same criterion to personal goodness by nature

The strongest personal proposal is also constitutive: the source's
goodness belongs to what it is, and its actual giving is a proper
exercise of intellectual appetite under the recipient's good. It does
not manufacture goodness by an arbitrary act of approval. Its additional
explanatory content is **apprehended practical specification**: the
producer understands the recipient's good and the relation of the means
to that good, and that understanding belongs to what directs its giving.

[ST I-II q.9 a.1](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/2009.htm#article1)
distinguishes exercise from specification by the object. Replies 1–3
retain nonnecessitation and distinguish practical presentation of good
from mere apprehension of truth. The [prior reviewed proper-manifestation argument](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/formal-logos/blob/5c0c0f22c5b8bb74f0a36fc40b43d17c4087777e/docs/notes/will-versus-chance.md#b-proper-manifestation-gives-a-stronger-personal-argument-than-ownership)
uses that distinction without transferring the finite soul's passage
from potency to act to the invariant source. It supports the positive
personal constitution; it does not make cognition alone its guarantee.

| Same explanatory criterion | Personal nature | Strictly noncognitive intrinsic nature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What makes D positively good for x? | Its warranted perfective content, grounded in the proposed nature/participation account; apprehension recognizes or practically presents that content. | The same warranted perfective content, grounded in the proposed intrinsic nature and derived recipient natures. |
| Does the standard require a superior separate existent? | Essential goodness denies that further dependency; the identity/constitution needs defense. | The same answer is available; its particular identity/constitution needs defense. |
| Why do the recipient's capacities and circumstances fit D? | Their understood relevance specifies the proper productive exercise together. | Their constitutive relevance specifies the proper joint manifestation together. |
| Is the positive good actually operative in production? | Required: giving must be the proper realization of the intellectual appetite under D, rather than an unrelated production accompanying knowledge. | Required: giving must manifest the intrinsically recipient-perfecting nature, rather than an unrelated production later evaluated as useful. |
| What explains source apprehension and practical uptake? | They are part of the proposed operation, if independently warranted. | It denies those source acts while retaining genuine uptake in the creature; it therefore does not explain an agreed datum of source uptake, if such a datum is established. |
| Why does the exercise occur, and why this eligible H? | Actual exercise and any warranted further contrastive ground are required. | Actual manifestation and the same level of contrastive ground are required. |
| Can the one nature meet simplicity and contingency? | The source application remains an obligation; the intellectual description is not itself a realization proof. | The source application remains an obligation; the intrinsic-final description is not itself a realization proof. |

There is a real explanatory difference in the fourth and fifth rows.
Intellectual appetite, properly exercised under D, has D's apprehended
content among its specifying conditions. A wholly blind operation cannot
be that very exercise in that respect. This strengthens the positive
personal account when its application is warranted. The present common
explanandum includes objective finality and rational recipients; it does
not already include the source's apprehended practical direction.
Counting the personal account's distinctive feature as a datum would
therefore assume the disputed application.

Conversely, giving both proposals the same primitive stopping permission
does not establish equal overall warrant. Familiar intentional activity
supports the intelligibility of the personal explanation. Familiar
intrinsic functional organization supports the intelligibility of some
local nature-based standards. Neither observation alone establishes an
original, strictly simple source of the relevant type. The current
standard-priority argument supplies no further discriminator between
those ultimate constitutions.

## The axiarchic alternative and its different source scope

A strong value-first account should not be replaced by indifferent
chance. In [John Leslie's interview with Kirk Lougheed](https://closertotruth.com/news/an-interview-with-john-leslie/),
the answer beginning "In philosophy of religion" distinguishes
value-guided creation from his stronger view that axiological
requiredness itself is creatively effective. On that stronger view,
even a divine person's existence would be explained by its value.
Leslie does not identify ethical requiredness here with a moral duty to
create oneself. His preferred wider picture includes infinite minds;
the interview therefore must not be cited as an endorsement of the
strictly noncognitive concrete source fixed in this investigation.

This is a serious way to deny that value needs a prior apprehender. Its
positive explanatory ambition is that the goodness of a realization
itself contributes to why it exists. It also changes what is ultimate:
if creative requiredness explains n, n is no longer the complete ultimate
explanatory source at that same scope. That is a consequence of the
proposed dependence, not an incoherence in every axiarchic worldview.
Nor does a standard's lack of concrete efficient agency refute a
proposal which assigns it a different kind of explanation.

For the fixed comparison, the more direct rival locates perfective
efficacy **in n's concrete nature**. It is axiarchic in the broad analyst
sense that objective value has productive explanatory relevance, but it
does not simply inherit Leslie's account or its modal consequences.
The concrete source is the producer, and its intrinsically good-directed
character explains the proper manifestation. If that character is only
an external norm renamed, the full-ground question returns. If it is
truly intrinsic, the prior-standard objection has not identified
anything outside n on which its direction depends.

Pure maximization would introduce a further choice of theory. A
necessary uniquely best realization plus a necessarily sufficient
actualizing principle can remove the stipulated contingency. A good
that admits several realizations does not by itself select one or prove
that nonnecessitating efficacy is coherent. The personal account faces
the corresponding issue if it makes perfect goodness necessitate the
unique best act. The present question neither assumes maximizing
axiarchism nor imports an escape through unexplained numerical chance.

## What would make the external-standard objection succeed?

The conditional objection is substantive when it identifies all of the
following, rather than passing directly from "objective" to "prior":

1. A determinate standard or principle S playing an independent
   constitutive role in n's direction, rather than merely a distinct
   description, our criterion for recognizing the same nature, or the
   recipient's downstream measure of fulfillment.
2. A defended real dependency: S constitutes, supplies, actualizes or
   sustains n's productive direction, or is an indispensable independent
   co-ground within the **claimed complete explanatory scope**.
3. A source-sufficiency premise that excludes that dependency at the
   selected scope. The exclusion cannot be borrowed from a definition
   concerning only actual concrete causal predecessors in a restricted
   relation.

Then n is not ultimate in the stipulated inclusive sense. This result
does not require that S be a mind, or entail that a personal source
escapes the same objection. If a personal source acquires its standard,
authority or direction from an equally independent S, the same
incompleteness applies. If it is allowed essential goodness, the
noncognitive candidate must be assessed for that possibility on its own
content rather than barred by the form of the standard argument.

The rival's proposed identity also has an exact failure condition. It
fails if the allegedly one nature can supply the specified production
only by presupposing a genuinely independent value-ground or received
actualizer, or if its evaluative description has no content beyond
whatever H occurs. The first is a dependency finding; the second is
an explanatory circularity. Neither follows merely from our needing
an evaluative premise to understand or warrant the account.

## What this test changes and what would revise it

**Scoped provisional judgment:** the prior-standard objection does not
currently exclude intrinsically good-directed impersonal nature. The
strongest response is positively constitutive, with real recipient
perfections and one proposed original productive orientation. It avoids
the asserted external-standard regress without making arbitrary actual
outputs good. This is progress beyond merely listing senses of priority.
It shows what the rival must claim and where a real failure would occur.

The account of original direction is still a proposal, not an established
source constitution. The goodness/form/end thesis, goodness-by-essence
language and local examples do not establish that n has the requisite
nature. The personal account has a distinctive positive account of
apprehended specification, but its source application is likewise not
established by normative objectivity alone. Parity on this particular
objection is not global explanatory equivalence or equal credence in
the complete worldviews.

The remaining discriminating work is concrete:

- **For an external-standard defeat:** identify S and the actual
  dependence by which it specifies the same n, and establish the
  inclusive explanatory scope that this violates. Merely rephrasing
  a constitutive truth as a prior law repeats the equivocation.
- **For the intrinsic proposal:** substantiate that the same positive
  recipient-perfecting character belongs to the actual source-owned
  power, explains its exercise and supplies K jointly, without a
  received actualizer or forbidden composition. Local standards alone
  are insufficient; a generic compatibility model would not answer
  this application question.
- **For a personal advantage:** warrant that this giving is the proper
  realization of intellectual appetite under D, or defend an
  independent argument showing that the same positive constitutive
  content necessarily involves source cognition. Mere apprehension,
  recipient rationality and successful effects leave the operative
  connection open.
- **For either account's reversal:** show a concrete mismatch between
  the claimed positive good and the recipient's actual perfection,
  an internal dependency excluded by the shared simplicity, or an
  exact modal consequence incompatible with the retained contingency.
  Apply the same result to both accounts wherever its premises match.

No additional axiom or lemma is proposed for this slice. The unresolved
questions concern the interpretation and warranted actuality of a
productive constitution. The existing frames and route-bridge theorem
already expose the extensional boundary. Adding unconstrained standard,
dependence and simplicity flags would not test the substantive proposed
identity or its original finality.

## Actual grounding, explanatory cycles and positive source constitution

This continuation tests the [owner's follow-up on #1998](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/formal-logos/pull/1998#issuecomment-7169154319).
Its proposals are research inputs, not previously certified conclusions.
The earlier review at `24b1c73b` does not cover this addition. The rival
retains an actual, strictly noncognitive, simple, necessary,

rational creatures. The comparison does not withdraw a grant because
the rival has not supplied a complete ultimate metaphysics.

### Actual specification does not require every norm to be fulfilled

Three claims must remain distinct: an evaluative proposition is true or
necessary; a particular creature or relation instantiates its conditions;
and an actual source-owned power or exercise is specified by that good.
A true obligation can remain violated. A genuine power can have a good
as its possible terminus without exercising it now. Neither case removes
the question of what actually constitutes the power's normative
specification. The question concerns the actual ground of this disposition
or exercise, not a rule requiring every true norm to have a successful
instance.

The mathematical comparison therefore does one limited job. A true
abstract characterization does not by itself introduce a concrete
producer, an actual power or its exercise. It also does not prove that
abstract normative content can have no explanatory role. If the claim is
that such content really specifies this actual source's power, that
relationship needs an account. On the intrinsic proposal, the source's
positive nature is the concrete realization of this specification;
the proposition articulates it without being an independently supplying
entity. On an external proposal, a distinct ground supplies something
indispensable to the specification. These are different constitutive
claims even if both theories express the same necessary proposition.

The point is not that n efficiently causes its own nature. Identity of
the productive and evaluative actuality is a proposed constitution,
not a strict self-grounding edge. Conversely, having one bearer does
not identify every fact about it: a fact about its nature and a fact
about its exercise may still require a substantive explanation of their
connection. Calling both facts intrinsic settles neither that connection
nor the simplicity of their realization.

Retain the earlier scope distinction concerning `ActualRoot`. A concrete
actual s which grounds n's existence in the selected `m.grounds` relation
contradicts that root predicate. A necessary proposition alone supplies
none of these premises. An independent indispensable ground of the
normatively specified exercise can defeat a claim that n completely
supplies its original productive specification without thereby defeating
existence-roothood. That second conclusion needs the wider explanatory
sufficiency claim and the actual dependence; it is not obtained by
renaming act-dependence as existence-grounding. The cycle implication and
any criterion factorization do not supply this missing interpretation.

### A real cycle requires the same facts in one explanatory order

Fix A as **this source's actual normatively specified giving or directing
exercise**, R as **the actual constitution of this recipient**, and D as
**the particular normative fact concerning this recipient**. Let
`prior x y` mean that fact x is explanatorily prior to fact y in one
transitive, irreflexive order. The proposed failure is then precise:

| Proposed edge | Required application, beyond its schematic description |
| --- | --- |
| A → R | This very exercise supplies this actual recipient constitution, with the productive dependence admitted into the selected explanatory order. |
| R → D | This actual constitution grounds this token normative fact in that same order. The claim must retain any additional normative ground needed; constitution by itself has not made a substantive evaluative principle true. |
| D → A | This token normative fact supplies the actuality or original specification of that same exercise, rather than its beneficial description, an observer's warrant, or the content of a possible end. |

Under those hypotheses, transitivity gives `prior A A`, contradicting
irreflexivity. Distinctness of the three labels is not needed for the
general implication. If two purportedly distinct roles are the very
same fact, the relevant strict edge is already problematic. If they
are different facts about the same bearer, bearer identity alone does
not eliminate the need to test the three edges.

The principal application risk lies in the last row. Let Aprod be the
productive occurrence and `Beneficial(Aprod)` the fact that it benefits
the recipient. The explanation
`Aprod → R → D → Beneficial(Aprod)` contains no return edge to Aprod.
The recipient's actual good can explain why the giving is rightly
described as beneficial. That is a legitimate downstream appraisal,
but it does not yet explain the giving's original direction. Likewise,
a relational description of giving *to this actually existing recipient*
must not be substituted for the source's intrinsic exercise and then
treated as an incoming actualizer of that exercise.

The first two edges need care as well. Efficient production, constitutive
grounding, normative explanation and conceptual dependence are not
automatically one relation. Their composition requires a justified
embedding into the chosen strict explanatory order, preserving the same
facts and edge directions. Three separately acyclic relations can have
a cyclic union. Nor is every use of "explains" a claim of strict priority:
mutual constraints and identity explanations need their own treatment.
This is why the cycle is a useful conditional failure, rather than a
general prohibition on mentioning the recipient's good in an explanation
of its production.

Eternity does not repair the exact cycle. No temporal succession occurs
in its proof, so making A, R and D timeless leaves `prior A A` unchanged.
Nor does calling the relation eternal establish any of its edges. The
substantive question is explanatory dependence, not which fact occurs
first in time. The merged [#1970 comparison](causal-perfection-transfer.md#does-intrinsically-grounded-self-understanding-require-real-articulation-2026-11-25-provisional)
already distinguishes priority between roles from strict priority between
their real referents; that result is reused here without rerunning its
role or act-coverage diagnostics.

**Executed finite control.** The owner's three-node cycle check was
reproduced and strengthened to enumerate every strict partial order on
the three labeled nodes. There are 6 total orders and 19 strict partial
orders; none contains all three cycle edges. Removing any one edge leaves
exactly one extension of each kind. Each singleton edge is separately a
strict partial order, while the three-edge union fails transitivity; its
transitive closure contains all three self-loops. The following is the
reproducible core of the executed check:

```python
from itertools import permutations, product

nodes = range(3)
cycle = ((0, 1), (1, 2), (2, 0))
pairs = tuple(product(nodes, repeat=2))

def strict_order(edges):
    return (all((x, x) not in edges for x in nodes)
            and all((x, z) in edges for x, y in edges
                    for y2, z in edges if y == y2))

def linear_extensions(edges):
    return [p for p in permutations(nodes)
            if all(p.index(x) < p.index(y) for x, y in edges)]

orders = [frozenset(p for p, bit in zip(pairs, bits) if bit)
          for bits in product((0, 1), repeat=9)]
orders = [order for order in orders if strict_order(order)]
assert len(orders) == 19
assert not linear_extensions(cycle)
assert not any(set(cycle) <= order for order in orders)
for i in range(3):
    remaining = cycle[:i] + cycle[i+1:]
    assert len(linear_extensions(remaining)) == 1
    assert sum(set(remaining) <= order for order in orders) == 1
assert all(strict_order(frozenset((edge,))) for edge in cycle)
assert not strict_order(frozenset(cycle))
closure = set(cycle)
while True:
    expanded = closure | {(x, z) for x, y in closure
                          for y2, z in closure if y == y2}
    if expanded == closure:
        break
    closure = expanded
assert closure == set(pairs)
```

This is finite relational verification. It supplies no actual source,
normative ground, philosophical edge or common-order interpretation.
The mixed-relation control diagnoses an invalid composition; it is not
a metaphysical model of the granted rival.

### The strongest intrinsic escape specifies a possible recipient good

Let E express the positive perfection appropriate to a possible rational
