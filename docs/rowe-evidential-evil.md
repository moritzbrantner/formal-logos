Rowe, “The Problem of Evil and Some Varieties of Atheism,” *American

## What the argument actually challenges

Rowe distinguishes narrow theism (a being jointly omniscient, omnipotent and
wholly good) from broader deity claims (p.335). His premises on p.336 are:

1. Some actual intense suffering could be prevented by an omniscient omnipotent
   being without unavoidable loss of a greater good or permission of equal or
   worse evil.
2. A wholly good omniscient being permits preventable intense suffering only
   under such a cost of prevention.

The conclusion is the denial of narrow theism. `POE-ROWE-DEDUCTIVE-CORE`
mechanically derives that consequence by instantiating the suffering witness
at a supposed being with all three attributes: the first premise excludes the
cost which the second requires. Costs have an outcome carrier and separate
counterfactual and comparative relations; the proof does not simply apply an
opaque implication with atheism already as its consequent. Neither premise is
proved true, and no Catholic root premise is discharged.

Rowe’s second premise is **necessary**, not sufficient, for morally permissible
permission (pp.336–337). Merely bringing some good out of suffering, or outweighing
it, need not suffice. Thus the existing Catholic general-evil consistency model
and q.2 a.3 ad 1’s `GodDrawsGoodFromEveryEvilReading` do not answer this argument:
that relation establishes neither unavoidable loss, unreplaceability, comparative
weight, nor an account of each actual suffering. The separate q.48 a.5 culpa cell
(#450) classifies voluntary operational defects conditionally; it explains neither
natural suffering nor divine permission.

The formal reading preserves two qualifications explicitly rather than quietly
strengthening the text:

- Footnote 2 excludes replaceable conjunctive goods (a good plus the suffering
  cannot manufacture necessity when the good alone is available). `GreaterGood`
  has this intended restriction; its truth in a real case is open.
- Footnote 3 allows justice as a good and imposes no later-in-time restriction.
  It also considers goods whose loss leaves the balance unchanged, admitting
  that the printed premise is somewhat too strong without this refinement.
  `POE-ROWE-BALANCE-NEUTRAL-CORE` also instantiates the proved structured core
  with this proposed refinement. It weakens the necessary permission constraint
  and correspondingly strengthens the factual gratuitous-suffering premise;
  both remain explicit and open. It neither proves the two formulations
  equivalent nor attributes the stronger simplified condition to every theist.

## Appearance, actuality and epistemic warrant

The fawn case (p.337) motivates the difference between lacking a recognized
justifying connection and knowing that no such connection exists. Rowe explicitly
accepts that appearance alone does not entail his first premise. His affirmative
case then invokes the range and profusion of suffering, its apparently weak
connection to goods, and the apparent availability of less suffering even when
some good is involved (pp.337–338, especially n.5). The strongest claim in this
article is defeasible rational support for premise (1), including support for
acceptance over suspension, rather than a demonstration or a numerical
probability theorem.

`ApparentlyPointlessSuffering` is only a limited finite-investigator projection
of the first appearance claim. It omits the distribution of suffering, background
information and rational warrant. `POE-ROWE-APPARENT-COMPATIBILITY` gives a concrete
finite interpretation with actual intense suffering, a qualifying theistic being,
an unrecognized unavoidable greater-good cost, and the necessary permission
constraint. It reuses the deductive-core result to exclude actual gratuitous
suffering in that interpretation. This establishes logical compatibility of this
limited appearance with narrow theism. It provides no probability, likelihood,
actual possibility, actual greater good, or sufficient moral license. In
particular, it is not a refutation of Rowe’s inductive argument.

The open warrant obligation is to specify which total evidence and epistemic
principles license moving from appearances to actual gratuitous suffering. A
proof that the inference is not deductively valid does not discharge it. No
probability premise is silently assumed or replaced by an opaque warrant bridge.
The candidate `ROWE-EVIL-TOTAL-EVIDENCE-WARRANT` preserves this gap.

## Shared answer map and the owner’s dilemma

The question remains `Q-MORAL-GROUNDING`: what makes the evaluative judgments
objective? The map is stored in these scoped argument/definition records and this
linked note, following #149’s existing set-of-propositions arrangement; no new
storage schema is introduced.

| Position/conditional cell | Available answer | Limit still open |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Catholic grounding and culpa | Existing privation account and conditional voluntary-operation classification (#160/#450) | No identification of suffering with culpa, no account showing all actual prevention has the required costs |

| Expressive/noncognitivist external argument | Existing emotivism result applies only when its exact moral-classification and truth/semantic premises are supplied | No Rowe-to-O’Connor sentence bridge or attribution is established here; descriptive suffering can remain true |
| Internal critic | Grant the theist’s evaluative standards conditionally and challenge permission under those standards | The critic need not adopt an independently grounded moral realism to pose the conditional inconsistency |

Applied conditionally, the owner’s dilemma says: if an external argument asserts
an objective evaluative premise, its complete rival worldview owes a grounding
answer; if it denies that very premise’s truth-aptness, it cannot simultaneously
use it as an objectively true external premise under the same semantics. This is
an obligation or a conditional inconsistency, not an automatic refutation of
Rowe. Merely lacking a grounding answer in this article is unexplained, not false.
Nor is an independent realist account refuted by its being non-theistic.

Keep four items separate: a natural suffering event; a voluntarily controlled
fault; an objective evaluative or permission judgment; and an expression of an
attitude. The existing theorem
`externalPremiseContradictsEmotivism` concerns a classified man-made-evil sentence
and explicit truth-aptness premises. None of its semantic bridges can be imported
merely because Rowe uses the word “evil.” His permission principle is the primary
evaluative pressure point (p.337 calls it a basic moral principle); the forest
fire’s occurrence by itself is not a voluntary fault. A hypothetical/internal
critique also survives the critic’s refusal to endorse objective morality as
an independent external commitment.

## Strong replies preserved, without resolving them by stipulation

Rowe’s own strongest theistic reply is the Moorean shift (pp.338–339): a believer
may accept the permission constraint, have independent rational grounds for a
theistic being, and consequently reject actual gratuitous suffering. Its logic
is the same cost contradiction used by the present theorem. Its epistemic force
depends on comparing those independent grounds with the suffering evidence, not
on declaring either first premise true. Rowe also distinguishes challenging the
sufficiency of the atheistic evidence from producing a specific actual good for
every event (p.338). His friendly atheism (pp.340–341) explicitly allows some
theists to be rationally justified while denying their belief’s truth.

As an open skeptical-theist reply, one can challenge whether finite investigators
should expect to recognize all morally relevant prevention costs. The non-theist
can answer that ordinary defeasible induction does not demand omniscience, that
the large distribution matters more than a single opaque case, and that possible
hidden goods alone do not establish such goods or erase the evidence. Broad
skepticism also owes an account of why everyday moral deliberation remains
reliable. These are preserved philosophical alternatives, not newly attributed
primary-source claims about later authors. No proposition about epistemic access,
priors, likelihoods, moral paralysis or total evidence is proved by the finite
model. A full comparison must keep these premises explicit.

## Premises and root effects

Intended and actual: no root-route change. The deductive result retains exactly
the actual-gratuitous-suffering premise and simplified necessary permission
constraint. It adds no third philosophical premise and removes neither. The refined
argument replaces both simplified premises together with their explicitly
qualified counterparts; it does not discharge either counterpart. The
existential model has no theorem hypotheses; its four recorded commitments are
satisfied only by the displayed interpretation. It establishes no premise about
the actual world. The source records establish what was read, not premise truth.
Issue #168 remains open for its other obligations and for the total-evidence warrant
recorded here. The six unavailable O’Connor exports are
untouched.

## Bounded recognition survey (analyst formulation, attribution deferred)

and supplies a finite list of candidate outcomes for each hypothetical preventer
and event. `Covered` requires every actual blocking outcome in the relevant
occurring intense-suffering cases to occur on that list. `Complete` requires
recognition of every listed actual blocking outcome in those cases. These are
separate open conditions, not definitions of omniscience in the hypothetical
preventer: investigator and preventer must not be confused. A listed justice
good could satisfy coverage; an unlisted unavoidable loss violates it. A listed

applicability of counterfactual costs and comparisons remains open as before.

Given both conditions, an apparent-pointlessness witness supplies an actual
gratuitous-suffering witness: any actual blocking outcome would be listed and

## Sense-by-sense objection audit (2026-11-17)

**Assessment.** Several familiar versions fail because they change the meaning
of evil or supply an unsupported prevention principle. The strongest surviving
challenge concerns *unjustified permission of actual suffering or deprivation*,
including a proposed deprivation of relationship. Its conditional logic survives
the audit; the contested work is establishing its actual witness and normative
conditions against the cumulative positive case. This is a narrower and stronger
opponent than “something bad happens, therefore no God.” It is not defeated by
changing the definition, requiring the critic to prove a complete metaethics, or
requiring deductive certainty for an evidential conclusion.

This is an analyst synthesis of the existing Aquinas, Rowe and objection-use
cells, not a new historical attribution or a newly checked theorem. Primary
anchors remain `AQUINAS-ST-I-Q48-A2`, `AQUINAS-ST-I-Q48-A3`,
`AQUINAS-ST-I-Q48-A5`, `AQUINAS-ST-I-Q2-A3`, and Rowe 1979 pp.335–339, with
the exact source scopes above. No source was newly materialized for this audit.
The following inventory keeps different kinds of distinction separate.

The existing inventory does **not** register either “a positive evil substance”
or “whatever contravenes divine will” as a new complete definition. They are
diagnostic contrasts below, not newly attributed schools or silently exhaustive
alternatives. Objective badness is a claim about evaluative truth, compatible
with several entries; it is not interchangeable with voluntary wrongdoing.

### 1. Mere limitation, privation and an alleged positive rival to God

**Strongest challenge.** If God is infinite goodness and the source of all
reality, why is there a real defect in anything caused by God? The q.2 a.3
objection compresses this into the claim that infinite good excludes all evil.
Its target is supreme goodness joined to power and universal causal dependence.

**Reply.** A creature's not possessing every possible perfection is not yet the
absence of a good due to that creature. The privation account distinguishes a
due capacity's failure from being finite. Nor does the positive reality of an
injuring process establish that its badness is a second, self-subsistent positive
principle opposed to God: actuality of a process and defect in its patient are
different predications. Goodness of an act *in one perfection* is not moral
approval of every use or consequence of that act.

This is substantive because it blocks the transfer from the source's unlimited
goodness to unlimited perfection in every effect. `Due x p` means a good apt for
the subject; it does not already mean “God must secure p without exception.”
That further prevention obligation must be argued. The existing
`POE-CATHOLIC-CONSISTENT-WITH-EVIL` interpretation and denial of the objection-one
conditional establish satisfiability/non-entailment at their encoded scope,
including the disputed stronger ad 1 premise. They do not establish a possible
world containing every attribute of the completed classical case.

**Verdict.** Evil inferred merely from finitude is misclassified; treating a
privation as an independent rival substance is unnecessary; the unconditional
exclusion principle does not follow from the recorded account. The reply does
not make blindness, injury or suffering unreal. `EvilIsAsPropositionalTruthReading`
expressly permits a true evil-exists sentence. A due-good defect still opens a
permission question, which must be answered on its merits.

### 2. Voluntary wrongdoing and natural-law evil

**Strongest challenge.** An omniscient, omnipotent good source allows agents to
commit grave wrongs it could stop. Why is the source not culpable for their
preventable consequences? The target is moral goodness in permission, not the
bare existence of a first cause.

**Reply.** The proximate agent's voluntary failure and God's causing or sustaining
the positive reality of an act are not the same fault. The existing culpa rule
requires a due operation under voluntary control and its absence or disorder;
it contains no theorem transferring a secondary agent's culpa to every cause
of that agent. Likewise “evil is to be avoided” in the practical first precept
does not immediately yield “every evil must be prevented by every capable
agent at any cost.” The subjects, actions, opportunities and relevant reasons
must be aligned.

Responsible agency supplies a positive explanatory candidate: actions that are
genuinely attributable to agents need not have their outcomes continuously
overridden. This is an analyst proposal, not a proof that freedom requires each
atrocity or that rescue destroys responsibility. The strongest critic can grant
freedom to choose wrongly while asking about prevention of its worst effects.
An adequate reply must compare that less costly alternative, not merely repeat
that freedom is valuable.

**Verdict.** The automatic attribution of every wrong to the first cause is
unsupported by the culpa definition. A general freedom response gives a reason
to distinguish doing wrong from permitting it, but does not defeat the strongest
permission objection. Classifying wrongdoing also leaves natural suffering
untouched; the latter needs no culpable creature as its immediate source.

### 3. Pain, suffering and natural evil

**Strongest challenge.** Severe human and animal suffering occurs with apparently
little benefit, including cases without voluntary wrongdoing. A perfect knower
and preventer seems able to preserve the goods with less suffering. Rowe's
forest-fire example belongs here; redescribing it as culpa changes the case.

**Reply.** A sensation's aversiveness, its objective badness for a subject, and
its being unjustifiably permitted are three different claims. A protective pain
can be bad in itself while serving health; that example defeats a universal
equation of unpleasantness with unjustifiability. It does not explain intense,
prolonged, or apparently useless pain. Similarly, regular embodied causation is
a candidate explanation of the availability of injury, not an established reason
for every duration, intensity or distribution of injury.

Omnipotence does not by itself make contradictory combinations feasible. But
calling a good “inseparable” from suffering is a claim that needs support;
ordinary physical dependence alone does not show that an omnipotent being lacks
a less costly way. The actual burden is the counterfactual comparison between
this suffering and its feasible prevention, as represented by `BlockingCost`.
The passage from “no wrongdoer caused this” to “nothing could justify allowing
it” is invalid; so is the opposite passage from “some suffering has a function”
to “this suffering is justified.”

**Verdict.** Suffering alone does not deductively exclude perfect power and
goodness. Neither the privation definition nor the freedom reply removes its
evidential force. A successful rebuttal must address the severity, distribution
and apparent alternatives invoked by the strongest version, not only the word
“evil.”

### 4. Dislike, truthlessness and objective badness

**Strongest external challenge.** “These events are objectively wrong or bad;
their permission conflicts with perfect goodness.” An internal version instead
asks whether the target's own standards and the admitted events cohere.

**Reply.** If the selected moral sentence is simultaneously denied truth-aptness
and asserted as the critic's own true premise, the exact four-bridge
`POE-EXTERNAL-PREMISE-VS-EMOTIVISM` result defeats that use. A mere expression of
dislike adds no objective divine duty by itself. This is a real inconsistency
in that stipulated use, not a rebuttal to all anti-realist reasoning. The checked
affective witness also blocks deriving truthlessness from expression alone.

Conversely, a critic who grants the target's objective standards conditionally
does not need an independent moral foundation to expose a conflict among them.
`EVIL-INTERNAL-PERMISSION-CHALLENGE` preserves exactly that use. An external realist
worldview can be compared on grounding, explanation and moral knowledge using
the existing [Railton/Boyd investigation](naturalistic-moral-realism.md); failure
to give a full theory in one argument does not make its evaluative premise false.
Requiring such a theory before admitting any objection would impose a burden
not required of ordinary moral knowledge.

Within the positive case, the privation account explains how objective defects
can depend on goods rather than constitute a rival ultimate. That is an
explanatory advantage to assess, not a proof that whoever recognizes evil has
already proved God. `STMT-GOODNESS-IS-ACTUALITY` is per-perfection coextension;
it does not by itself derive moral perfection, Rowe's comparative cost standard,
or every divine permission rule.

**Verdict.** The exact truthless/own-true-premise combination is excluded. Broad
“the atheist cannot call anything evil” and “objective evil immediately proves
God” shortcuts are unsupported. The strongest internal and realist challenges
remain available without changing their meanings.

### 5. “Contrary to God's will”

This is an analyst disambiguation, not an added corpus definition. If “will”
means an efficacious intention that this very event not occur, that intention
plus its occurrence conflicts with efficacy. The material question is whether
perfect goodness entails that intention in every evil case. It is not answered
by naming the event evil. If instead the phrase means contrary to a command or
to the due order God approves for agents, violation of that order does not by
itself contradict permission of the violation. Positive willing of a defect,
permitting it, commanding against it and causing the positive being of an agent
are different relations. The existing `DEF-AD1-PERMISSION-OF-EVILS`
(`b4d64cab-bf94-5cd2-91cc-190b717c6e61`) explicitly leaves permission distinct from
production and justification.

**Verdict.** The apparent immediate contradiction equivocates unless the same
efficacious intention is established. Once repaired, the objection returns to
whether the particular permission has a sufficient reason. This audit supplies
no universal divine-will theorem and does not identify disobedience with natural
evil.

### 6. Apparent and actual gratuitousness

**Strongest challenge.** The scale, pattern and apparent avoidability of suffering
support the judgment that at least one actual event lacks the relevant prevention
cost. That is stronger than claiming that an unseen reason is deductively absent.

**Reply to the deductive version.** `POE-ROWE-APPARENT-COMPATIBILITY` refutes the
transition from the recorded limited appearance predicate to actual absence of
cost. The recognition survey locates sufficient additional coverage and
recognition conditions. Neither establishes that a real investigator must be
omniscient before making a reasonable inference.

**Reply to the evidential version.** The force of “we see no reason” depends on
how likely investigators would be to recognize the relevant reason if there
were one. Local knowledge of injuries can support competent prevention decisions
without equal competence concerning every globally related counterfactual good.
That asymmetry is a focused possible undercutter; it needs a defended account
of its domain. A universal distrust of evaluative judgment would also threaten
the goodness and permission premises used by the defense. Logical compatibility
alone supplies no likelihood and does not erase surprising evidence.

The positive routes matter here. Rowe's Moorean shift is licensed conditionally:
if the cumulative grounds for a wholly good, omniscient, omnipotent being and the
relevant permission rule outweigh the support for gratuitousness, rejecting the
latter can be reasonable without identifying each divine reason. Neither side
gets to ignore the other's evidence. Shared assumptions among positive routes
must not be counted as independent confirmations; likewise repeated descriptions
of one suffering case are not independent observations. This audit supplies no
numerical prior, likelihood or overall posterior.

**Verdict.** The appearance-to-actuality entailment fails; the evidential inference
remains defeasible and contested. If the exact actual-cost premise
`STMT-ROWE-GRATUITOUS-SUFFERING` (`8eebfefd-e24a-52f0-bf85-5ced6b569eae`) and
permission premise `STMT-ROWE-PERMISSION-CONSTRAINT`
(`0a95a3c3-5899-5350-9fef-6e88e79819e8`) are warranted, their checked conclusion
`STMT-ROWE-NO-THEISTIC-BEING` (`ebcd1744-7af1-53c1-987e-6cd56a41f118`) stands.
One cannot refute that conditional by saying evil is privation. Nor is a
blocking cost automatically a sufficient moral justification: the argument
uses its absence as a necessary-condition failure. The balance-neutral sibling
must be compared with both of its adjusted premises, not mixed into the
simplified pair opportunistically.

### Cumulative finding and next discriminating tests

The audit removes unconditional all-evil exclusion, automatic transfer of
creaturely fault, strict truthlessness combined with an own-true moral premise,
and deductive conversion of the limited appearance predicate. These are scoped
gains for the defense. It also preserves objective evil and finite moral
knowledge rather than denying the phenomena to protect the conclusion. The
remaining comparison is between actual unjustified permission and positive
grounds for a perfectly good source. The existence routes therefore retain
their existing warrant; this audit supplies neither a new positive proof nor
a blanket defeat of the strongest evil objection.

The next tests should discriminate between these surviving accounts:

1. **Permission norm.** Compare Rowe's simplified and balance-neutral necessary
   conditions with the intended classical goodness claim. Specify whether the
   proposed good or obligation belongs to the sufferer, another agent or the
   whole order, and whether its loss is unavoidable. Do not upgrade production
   of any good into sufficient justification.
2. **Actual case and alternatives.** Choose an already recorded suffering case;
   assess the claimed lesser-harm alternative and the strongest positive reason
   for not taking it. The relevant difference is an actual reason or warranted
   ignorance about it, not a newly invented possible story.
3. **Evidential sensitivity.** State what this investigator could reasonably
   expect to detect, and how the distribution changes the inference. Compare
   that limited skepticism with the ordinary moral judgments both sides retain.
   This serves the existing `ROWE-EVIL-TOTAL-EVIDENCE-WARRANT` obligation.
4. **Root attributes.** Test the already identified common-carrier bridge from
   `STMT-UNIQUE-GOD-WITH-CLASSICAL-ATTRIBUTES-EXISTS`
   (`20a2b6a3-83e7-5ea0-be63-9dbc03c302b3`) to Rowe's joint attribute predicate.
   An unresolved semantic bridge limits the registered root connection; it is
   not itself a philosophical answer to an identified target's permission.
5. **Relational deprivation.** Apply the [hiddenness mapping](hiddenness.md#hiddenness-as-a-special-case-of-evil-2026-11-17)
   at one aligned actual person/time, comparing modest access with the best
   proposed reason for delay. Count overlapping evil/hiddenness evidence jointly.
